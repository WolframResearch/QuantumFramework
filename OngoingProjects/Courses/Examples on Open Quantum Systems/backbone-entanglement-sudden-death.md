# Backbone: Entanglement Sudden Death Under Independent Local Noise

This page is the statement layer of a planned essay on the sudden death of entanglement (Yu and Eberly, Phys. Rev. Lett. 93, 140404 (2004); Science 323, 598 (2009)): a graph of assumptions, choices, and derived statements, each given in words and formulas only. Every node names its parents, so the dependency graph is recoverable from the page, and the layers order the reading from the setup to the headline and then to what holds when an assumption is weakened. Three conventions hold throughout. The page has two layers: a construction layer, where the physical roots are translated into the equation S0 by cited standard theory, and an analysis layer, where every consequence consumes S0 as a contract and therefore survives any construction that yields the same equation; the equation is never a root here, because the weakenings below (a shared bath, a change of the channel's direction) reach beneath it. Every assumption must be consumed by at least one statement that is not its own weakening; a root failing that test is not an assumption of the theory but only a direction along which the model can be varied. And every statement's tail separates provenance (derived in place, cited, or conjectured) from verification (exact decision, analytic cross-check, numerical check, or none), naming the clause scope and what the checking route shares with the finding route. Node labels: R is a root, a setup assumption; C is a choice, a meaning we fixed rather than a fact; S is a derived statement, with S0 reserved for the equation of motion (E would name it if posited as a contract without its construction, which this page does not do); H is the headline, the terminal statement; N is a recorded non-assumption; a prime marks the same statement under a weakened root. Basis conventions: $|0\rangle$ is the ground level, the two-qubit basis is ordered $|00\rangle, |01\rangle, |10\rangle, |11\rangle$, and an X state carries populations $a, b, c, d$ on that diagonal with coherences $w$ between $|00\rangle$ and $|11\rangle$ and $z$ between $|01\rangle$ and $|10\rangle$. Nothing here executes, and no companion essay exists yet: the verifications named in the tails were run as exact scratch computations during drafting and are collected, runnable, in *backbone-entanglement-sudden-death-checks.wl* beside this page; the full manifests migrate to the companion when it is written.

## The setup, and the choice

**R1 (assumption).** Each qubit couples to its own environment, and the two environments share nothing: no common mode, no cross-correlation, and the qubits do not interact, directly or through anything they both touch. Two different kinds of claim share this line: an existence claim (each qubit has a local loss channel) and a closure claim (the list of couplings ends there); the closure is what S0′ weakens. The three bath roots are pairwise separable by corner models: a collective bath keeps R2 and R3 and drops R1 (S0′), local dephasing keeps R1 and R3 and drops R2 (S2′), and a structured local bath keeps R1 and R2 and drops R3 (the pinned corner in R3).

**R2 (assumption).** Each environment is at zero temperature and absorbs energy: the local motion drains the excited level $|1\rangle$ toward the ground level $|0\rangle$ and never re-excites. This fixes the direction of the local motion; the mirror model, a bath that only excites, is this same page under the relabeling $\hat\sigma_x\otimes\hat\sigma_x$ of both qubits, so what the direction really fixes is which pure product state is the channel's fixed point. Both qubits drain at one common rate $\gamma$, a simplification adopted for the closed forms below, not a normalization: the rate ratio is dimensionless and cannot be scaled away, and S2 records that the death criteria survive unequal rates, which enter only through the death time. The corner with temperature restored is pinned for the record and consumed by nothing below: at any nonzero temperature every entangled two-qubit X state dies at finite time (Al-Qasimi and James, Phys. Rev. A 77, 012117 (2008)), so the discrimination among initial states in S2 and H is itself a zero-temperature statement.

**R3 (assumption).** Each environment is memoryless: its correlation time lies far below $1/\gamma$, so the loss proceeds at the single constant rate and the future depends only on the present state. The corner with memory restored is pinned for the record and consumed by nothing below: under a structured local reservoir the death studied here is followed by revivals of entanglement (Bellomo, Lo Franco and Compagno, Phys. Rev. Lett. 99, 160502 (2007)).

**R4 (assumption).** The initial state lies in the X class of the shared energy basis: populations $a, b, c, d$ on the four levels, coherence only between $|00\rangle$ and $|11\rangle$ (magnitude $w$) and between $|01\rangle$ and $|10\rangle$ (magnitude $z$). The class contains every Bell state, is closed under both channels considered below, and its entanglement has a closed algebraic form. The restriction is convenience, not necessity: a necessary and sufficient sudden-death criterion exists for general two-qubit states (Huang and Zhu, Phys. Rev. A 76, 062322 (2007)); no weakening of R4 is offered on this page. Two pure one-parameter probes are used throughout: $|\Phi\rangle = \alpha|00\rangle + \beta|11\rangle$ and $|\Psi\rangle = \alpha|01\rangle + \beta|10\rangle$, with $\alpha^2 + \beta^2 = 1$ and $\alpha, \beta > 0$. R4 is separable from the bath roots trivially: the same baths accept any initial state, and the same family can be handed to any channel.

**C1 (choice).** "The entanglement dies" is used here for exactly one claim: the concurrence reaches zero at a finite time and stays there. Two neighboring claims are inequivalent to it and to each other, and the page keeps them apart. First, local decay: every one-qubit quantity relaxes smoothly and only asymptotically (S1), so nothing local ever dies in the above sense. Second, loss of all quantum correlation: along trajectories of exactly this kind, quantum discord decays only asymptotically even where entanglement dies (Werlang, Souza, Fanchini and Villas-Boas, Phys. Rev. A 80, 024103 (2009)), so the death of entanglement is not the death of correlation. A third boundary is the observer: the score below is computed from the full two-qubit state, and an observer with limited measurements sees an effective entanglement that can die sooner, even for states whose entanglement never dies (Roszak, Horodecki and Horodecki, Phys. Rev. A 81, 042308 (2010)); the death times below belong to the full state. The score itself is a choice: concurrence rather than negativity. For two qubits the choice is provably harmless as far as death is concerned: a two-qubit state is separable exactly when its partial transpose has no negative eigenvalue (Peres, Phys. Rev. Lett. 77, 1413 (1996); Horodecki, Horodecki and Horodecki, Phys. Lett. A 223, 1 (1996)), concurrence vanishes exactly on the separable states (Wootters, Phys. Rev. Lett. 80, 2245 (1998)), and negativity, zero exactly on the states with positive partial transpose, vanishes exactly there as well. Every score that is zero precisely on the separable set assigns the same death time, the hitting time of that set; the scores disagree only about how the approach is graded, never about when it ends. *Cited as above; the negativity clause derived in place from the cited criterion. Verification: on the X class the coincidence of the concurrence zero set with the positive-partial-transpose set is an exact decision recorded at S1; the general two-qubit statements are imported from their sources, no check here.*

## The equation, derived from the setup

**S0 (from R1, R2, R3; cited).** Standard open-system theory turns the setup into
$$\dot\rho = \gamma\left(\mathcal D[\hat\sigma_-\otimes\mathbb 1]\rho + \mathcal D[\mathbb 1\otimes\hat\sigma_-]\rho\right), \qquad \mathcal D[\hat c]\rho = \hat c\rho\hat c^\dagger - \tfrac12\left(\hat c^\dagger\hat c\,\rho + \rho\,\hat c^\dagger\hat c\right),$$
with $\hat\sigma_- = |0\rangle\langle 1|$, and each root visible in the equation's shape: R1 is the generator being a sum of two strictly one-sided terms with no cross term coupling the qubits; R2 is the jump operator being the lowering operator alone, with no raising term $\mathcal D[\hat\sigma_+]$; R3 is the single constant rate. Three further premises live inside the cited translation rather than in the root list: an initially factorized system-environment state, weak coupling, and the rotating-wave step. They become roots of this page the moment any planned weakening reaches them; until then they are part of what the citation is trusted for, and the shape claim above is a visibility statement, not a completeness proof. *Cited: Breuer and Petruccione, The Theory of Open Quantum Systems (Oxford 2002), chapter 3. Verification: none; the translation is trusted to its source, and every scratch check below computes with exactly this equation.*

## Consequences

**S1 (from S0, R4, C1).** The equation integrates to a channel that factorizes into the same one-qubit map on each qubit: amplitude damping with damped fraction $p = 1 - e^{-\gamma t}$, Kraus pair $K_0 = \mathrm{diag}(1, \sqrt{1-p})$ and $K_1 = \sqrt p\,|0\rangle\langle 1|$. On the X class the channel is six algebraic flows,
$$a \to a + p\,(b + c) + p^2 d, \quad b \to (1-p)(b + p d), \quad c \to (1-p)(c + p d), \quad d \to (1-p)^2 d, \quad w \to (1-p)\,w, \quad z \to (1-p)\,z,$$
so the X form is preserved, and local phase rotations set $w, z \ge 0$ once and for all (they are local unitaries, and the score is invariant under local unitaries: Wootters, cited in C1). Every matrix element is a polynomial in $e^{-\gamma t}$: the motion is analytic in time and reaches its limit, all population at $|00\rangle$, only asymptotically. Locally there is even less to watch: on the X class the marginals are diagonal at all times, so each qubit's local content is populations only, decaying as $e^{-\gamma t}(c+d)$ and $e^{-\gamma t}(b+d)$; a lone qubit prepared in superposition, a state outside R4's class, under the same one-qubit channel keeps its coherence proportional to $e^{-\gamma t/2}$, so the channel itself does nothing sudden either. Finally, the score has a closed form on the class,
$$C(\rho) = 2\max\left(0,\; w - \sqrt{bc},\; z - \sqrt{ad}\right),$$
and it vanishes exactly where the partial transpose stays positive, since the partial transpose of an X state merely swaps the two coherences between its two 2 by 2 blocks. Positivity of an X state is exactly the pair of block conditions $w^2 \le ad$ and $z^2 \le bc$ (with nonnegative populations), so at most one branch of the maximum can be positive, the two coherences unable to both exceed their cross-sector geometric means (the observation is Terra Cunha's, New J. Phys. 9, 237 (2007)). *Provenance by clause: the map and the smoothness derived in place (Kraus composition, read off); the Kraus pair and the closed form cited: Nielsen and Chuang, Quantum Computation and Quantum Information (Cambridge 2000), chapter 8, and Yu and Eberly, Quantum Inf. Comput. 7, 459 (2007). Verification, exact decisions in a scratch kernel: the map equals the Kraus sum on a fully symbolic X state (Simplify to the zero matrix) and, with $p = 1-e^{-\gamma t}$, satisfies S0 identically (FullSimplify to the zero matrix), two routes sharing only the kernel's algebra; the closed form: the characteristic polynomial of $\rho\tilde\rho$ factors symbolically into the four claimed roots $(\sqrt{ad}\pm w)^2$ and $(\sqrt{bc}\pm z)^2$, the max form follows by Reduce under positivity (True), and three exact rational X states were pushed through Wootters' eigenvalue recipe directly (RootReduce agreement), an independent route; the partial-transpose coincidence and the at-most-one-branch lemma by Resolve over the reals (True); the local decay laws by Simplify (zero).*

**S2 (from S1, R4).** Each branch of the score factors under the map, with each qubit's damped fraction allowed its own value:
$$w(t) - \sqrt{b(t)\,c(t)} = \sqrt{(1-p_1)(1-p_2)}\left[\,w - \sqrt{(b + p_1 d)(c + p_2 d)}\,\right], \qquad z(t) - \sqrt{a(t)\,d(t)} = \sqrt{(1-p_1)(1-p_2)}\left[\,z - \sqrt{d\left(a + p_2 b + p_1 c + p_1 p_2 d\right)}\,\right].$$
The envelope multiplies the coherence and the population term alike, so survival is decided inside the bracket: there the coherence stands still while the channel feeds the term under the square root from the populations in transit, and each bracket only decreases. Death is therefore a comparison of the initial coherence with the ceiling its population term will reach as both fractions run to one. Entangled through the outer coherence: death at finite time exactly when
$$w^2 < (b + d)(c + d);$$
entangled through the inner coherence (the trace being one): death exactly when
$$z^2 < d.$$
Both rates must actually run: for any pair of strictly positive rates the criteria are rate-independent, the rates entering only through the death time (equal rates buying its closed form), but silence one bath and the reachable ceiling shrinks, so a state marked for death can survive forever. One exact witness: $a = \tfrac12$, $b = \tfrac1{20}$, $c = \tfrac1{10}$, $d = \tfrac7{20}$, $w^2 = \tfrac1{10}$ dies under every pair of positive rates yet outlives the criterion with the second bath off. When the crossing happens it is transversal (strictly negative slope for $d > 0$) and permanent. The probes specialize this cleanly. For $|\Phi\rangle$, where $b = c = 0$, $d = \beta^2$, $w = \alpha\beta$: death exactly when $\beta > \alpha$, at
$$p_d = \frac{\alpha}{\beta}, \qquad t_d = \frac1\gamma \ln\frac{\beta}{\beta - \alpha}, \qquad C(t) = 2\max\!\left(0,\; e^{-\gamma t}\beta\left[\alpha - \beta\left(1 - e^{-\gamma t}\right)\right]\right),$$
the balanced Bell state $\alpha = \beta$ being the marginal case, $C = e^{-2\gamma t}$, asymptotic. For $|\Psi\rangle$, and for every X state with no doubly excited population: loss cannot create an excitation, so $d(t) = 0$ exactly, $z^2 < d$ is unreachable, and the state stays entangled forever; the single-excitation Bell states decay as $C = e^{-\gamma t}$. Two inversions are the point. A state with more weight on the doubly excited level can be the more entangled one and still be the one that dies: for $\beta < 1/\sqrt2 < \beta' < \sqrt{1-\beta^2}$ the primed state carries more concurrence at $t = 0$ yet dies at finite time while the other never does, because the term under the square root is fed by exactly the population whose amplitude carries the coherence. And dying at finite time is not a function of the initial state's local-unitary invariants, although the entanglement is one: $\hat\sigma_x\otimes\hat\sigma_x$ exchanges $\alpha$ and $\beta$, so of a local-unitary pair of equally entangled initial states with $\alpha \neq \beta$ exactly one member dies. Nor does doubly excited population alone condemn a state: an inner-entangled mixture with $z^2 \ge d$ lives forever beside it (one exact witness: $b = c = z = 9/20$, $d = 1/10$, $a = w = 0$). *Provenance by clause: the two criteria are cited, Ali, Alber and Rau, J. Phys. B 42, 025501 (2009), following Huang and Zhu, Phys. Rev. A 76, 062322 (2007), and rederived here from S1; the finite-temperature form of the same continuity argument is Al-Qasimi and James, Phys. Rev. A 77, 012117 (2008), these criteria being its zero-temperature endpoint; the persistence at $d = 0$ and the local-unitary observation are in Terra Cunha, New J. Phys. 9, 237 (2007); the probe closed forms and the boundary witness derived in place. Verification, exact decisions in a scratch kernel, per clause: both branch factorizations with independent damped fractions by Simplify (zero); both death criteria by Resolve over the reals in polynomial form (True), the outer one directly with independent fractions, the inner one through the diagonal case plus the domination of the radicand by its diagonal value (both True); monotonicity of every radicand, hence permanence, by Resolve (True); transversality at any crossing with $d > 0$, on both branches with positivity kept, by Resolve (True); the $\Phi$ death region exactly $p \ge \alpha/\beta$, the iff against $\beta > \alpha$, the slope $-\beta(\beta - \alpha)$ at $p_d$, positivity of the evolved family, the death time formula, and the Bell-point specializations by Reduce, Resolve or Simplify on exact symbolic input (all True); the survivor witness checked exactly (valid state, entangled, never dying); the weight exchange under $\hat\sigma_x\otimes\hat\sigma_x$ by direct matrix identity; the strictly-positive-rates boundary by exact witness, dying under free fractions and surviving with one bath silent, both by Resolve (True), a clause the first draft lacked until review produced the counterexample; the entanglement-versus-death comparison likewise corrected by review, which refuted the first draft's unscoped version and decided the scoped one by Resolve (True). The checking route shares S1's map and score; the route-independent leg is the partial transpose: its negative eigenvalue vanishes at the same $p_d$, by Resolve (True).*

## The headline

**H (from S2, C1).** Under strictly local, smooth, forever-acting loss, entanglement can end while nothing local ends. The ending is a statement about the pair's reduced state: what the qubits lose the traced-out environments gain, reservoir entanglement born suddenly as the qubits' dies (Lopez, Romero, Lastra, Solano and Retamal, Phys. Rev. Lett. 101, 080503 (2008)). Every matrix element of the evolved state is a polynomial in $e^{-\gamma t}$ and reaches its limit only asymptotically, each qubit alone relaxes smoothly with excited population proportional to $e^{-\gamma t}$, yet for $\alpha|00\rangle + \beta|11\rangle$ with $\beta > \alpha$ the concurrence is exactly zero from $t_d = \gamma^{-1}\ln[\beta/(\beta - \alpha)]$ onward. Mechanism, the geometric reading of sudden death (Terra Cunha, New J. Phys. 9, 237 (2007)): the suddenness is not in the motion but in what is asked of it. Separable states fill a closed set of nonzero volume (Zyczkowski, Horodecki, Sanpera and Lewenstein, Phys. Rev. A 58, 883 (1998)), the concurrence is zero exactly on that set (C1), and the smooth trajectory crosses its boundary transversally; the kink of $\max(0,\cdot)$ records the crossing while the analytic matrix elements register nothing. The nonzero volume makes a finite-time crossing possible; it does not make it compulsory. Whether a given trajectory crosses is decided jointly by where the state starts and which way the channel drains: the same loss leaves $\alpha|01\rangle + \beta|10\rangle$, the members of the same family with $\beta \le \alpha$, and every inner-entangled state with $z^2 \ge d$ entangled forever (S2), while any pair of strictly positive rates leaves the verdict unchanged, the death point in damped fraction being fixed by the initial state alone (S2 records the boundary: one bath silenced can flip it). And the discrimination is itself a zero-temperature statement: warm the baths and death becomes universal (the corner pinned at R2). Death is sudden, local decay never is, and which entangled states die is set by geometry and direction, not by speed. Audit: H rests on the contract S0 together with R4 and C1, through S1 and S2; S0 is realized from R1, R2, R3 by the cited translation, so the analysis survives any construction that yields the same equation. The geometric reading, the nonzero-volume clause, and the sudden-birth clause are imported from their citations. No conjecture anywhere in the closure. *Derived in place from S2 reread; the mechanism clause cited: Terra Cunha as above. Verification: through S2's exact decisions and the partial-transpose cross-route; the volume clause cited, no check here.*

## When an assumption is weakened

**S2′ (from R2 weakened, with R1, R3, R4 kept; S1).** Replace each absorber by a memoryless dephaser, $\dot\rho = \tfrac\Gamma2\left(\mathcal D[\hat\sigma_z\otimes\mathbb 1] + \mathcal D[\mathbb 1\otimes\hat\sigma_z]\right)\rho$: the channel again factorizes, the populations freeze, and both coherences decay, $w, z \to e^{-2\Gamma t}(w, z)$. The same score and the same kind of kink, but the roles in S2's bracket reverse: the population term under the square root is now frozen while the coherence falls past it, so in the sector $z = 0$ an initially entangled state dies at finite time exactly when that term is nonzero, at $e^{-2\Gamma t_d} = \sqrt{bc}/w$, and a state with $bc = 0$ never dies. Which states die therefore changes with the channel's direction. Under loss the pure states with $\beta > \alpha$ die while no $d = 0$ state can; under dephasing no pure superposition dies at all ($b = c = 0$ for $\Phi$, $ad = 0$ for $\Psi$: nothing under either square root), while the mixture $F|\Phi_+\rangle\langle\Phi_+| + \tfrac{1-F}2\left(|01\rangle\langle01| + |10\rangle\langle10|\right)$, entangled exactly for $F > \tfrac12$, dies for every $F$ strictly between $\tfrac12$ and $1$, at $e^{-2\Gamma t_d} = (1-F)/F$; at $F = 1$ it is the pure Bell state and survives, consistently. What survives the change of direction is the shape of the phenomenon, a smooth trajectory crossing the separable boundary with a kink in the score; what does not survive is the list of which states cross. This weakening doubles as the corner model separating R2 from R3: memoryless without absorption. *Derived in place from S1's closed form with the dephasing map; sudden death under dephasing is in Yu and Eberly, Quantum Inf. Comput. 7, 459 (2007). Verification, exact decisions in a scratch kernel: the dephasing map and its generator identity by Simplify and FullSimplify (zero); frozen populations (zero); the sector death criterion, its finiteness, and the survival of the $bc = 0$ sector by Resolve (True); no pure superposition dies, in either sector, by Resolve (True); the witness family's validity, its entanglement threshold at $F = \tfrac12$, and its death point by Resolve (True). The route shares S1's closed form; no route-independent check was run here.*

**S0′ (from R1 weakened, with R2 and R3 kept).** Let both qubits drain into one shared zero-temperature memoryless environment through the collective operator $\hat J_- = \hat\sigma_-\otimes\mathbb 1 + \mathbb 1\otimes\hat\sigma_-$, so $\dot\rho = \gamma\,\mathcal D[\hat J_-]\rho$. The change reaches beneath the equation, which is why this page exposes S0's construction instead of positing a contract. And the physics changes in kind, not in degree: the singlet $(|01\rangle - |10\rangle)/\sqrt2$ is annihilated by $\hat J_-$ and is therefore stationary, a maximally entangled state that the shared zero-temperature bath never touches, protected by an antisymmetry the independent-bath model cannot see; under R1 the same state decays as $C = e^{-\gamma t}$ (S2). The protection is a degeneracy statement: give the qubits unequal splittings and the free Hamiltonian rotates the singlet into the symmetric combination, which the collective operator damps, so stationarity holds exactly when $\omega_1 = \omega_2$ (or no splitting at all); the corner model carries a resonance condition the independent-bath model never needed (N). Nor is death under the shared bath permanent: on the $\Phi$ family the collective equation solves in closed form and the outer branch carries the factor $\alpha e^{\gamma t} - \beta\gamma t$, negative somewhere exactly when $\alpha/\beta < \max_{u>0} u\,e^{-u} = 1/e$ yet positive again at large times, so the concurrence can sit at zero for a finite interval and then revive with no memory anywhere in the model: dark periods and revivals are Ficek and Tanas, Phys. Rev. A 74, 024304 (2006). Under R1 the crossing was permanent (S2); the shared bath takes even that, so R3's memory corner is not the only road back from death. A shared bath can also build entanglement between qubits that never meet (Braun, Phys. Rev. Lett. 89, 277901 (2002); Ficek and Tanas, Phys. Rep. 372, 369 (2002)). R1 is thus a closure root in earnest: adding a coupling, not strengthening one, reverses conclusions, and the deletion of "shared" from the setup is load-bearing for every death statement above. *Derived in place for the dark-state and degeneracy clauses. Verification, exact decisions: $\hat J_-|\Psi_-\rangle = 0$ and $\mathcal D[\hat J_-]\left(|\Psi_-\rangle\langle\Psi_-|\right) = 0$ symbolically (zero, exactly); the stationarity condition with splittings by Reduce, returning exactly $\omega_1 = \omega_2$; the shared-bath closed form on the $\Phi$ family solves its equation identically (FullSimplify, zero, trace one); the dark-period condition derived in place from the branch factor, the maximum of $u e^{-u}$ located by its derivative's sign change, each piece checked exactly; the dark interval and the revival checked on one exact family member by sign evaluation at exact times, other members untested. The entanglement-creation clause is cited, verification none here.*

**N (a recorded non-assumption).** No resonance or stillness assumption appears in the independent-bath model, on purpose. Give each qubit its own energy splitting, $\hat H = -\tfrac{\omega_1}2\hat\sigma_z\otimes\mathbb 1 - \tfrac{\omega_2}2\mathbb 1\otimes\hat\sigma_z$ (the signs place each excited level above its ground level in this basis), equal or not: as superoperators the Hamiltonian commutes with the local loss generator, so the full solution is S1's solution conjugated by a diagonal phase unitary, and every population, every coherence magnitude, hence every statement derived from S0, is unchanged. The boundary of the deletion: any local Hamiltonian diagonal in the shared energy basis is harmless to the independent-bath statements; the collective corner S0′ sits outside the deletion and carries its own degeneracy condition, recorded there; a transverse local drive leaves the X class and is a different problem, and a qubit-qubit interaction would violate R1 rather than this deletion. *Derived in place (a diagonal unitary multiplies each matrix element by a unit phase). Verification, exact decisions: the commutator of the two generators vanishes on a fully symbolic 4 by 4 matrix (zero); the rotated damped solution satisfies the full equation identically (FullSimplify, zero); the coherence moduli agree with the undriven ones (zero); the energy ordering under the chosen signs checked exactly.*

## The abstract, read off the graph

Two qubits, each draining into its own zero-temperature memoryless environment, evolve by the same local loss applied to each side. On X states in the energy basis the motion is six algebraic flows, every matrix element a polynomial in $e^{-\gamma t}$, so nothing in the state, and nothing on either qubit alone, ever reaches its endpoint at finite time. The entanglement can. The concurrence of an X state is a maximum of zero and two coherence-minus-population branches, at most one of which can be live, and local loss feeds each population term from the levels in transit while only scaling the coherence, so an initially entangled state dies at finite time exactly when its coherence falls short of the ceiling its population term will reach: $w^2 < (b+d)(c+d)$ through the outer coherence, $z^2 < d$ through the inner one, for any pair of strictly positive rates. For $\alpha|00\rangle + \beta|11\rangle$ that is $\beta > \alpha$, with death at $p_d = \alpha/\beta$ of the damped fraction, while $\alpha|01\rangle + \beta|10\rangle$ never dies, and of a local-unitary pair of equally entangled $\alpha|00\rangle + \beta|11\rangle$ states with $\alpha \neq \beta$ exactly one dies. The suddenness lives in the score's kink at the boundary of the separable set, a closed set of nonzero volume that a smooth trajectory can cross transversally, not in the dynamics, which stays analytic throughout. What the pair loses, the traced-out environments gain. Dephasing instead freezes the populations and lets the coherence fall past them, so the dying set reshuffles: no pure superposition dies, while outer-sector mixtures with both single-excitation levels occupied always do. Warm the baths and the discrimination disappears: every entangled state dies at finite time. A shared bath, the one deleted coupling, changes the physics in kind: the singlet becomes stationary, granted degenerate splittings, and a bath can create entanglement. Local energy splittings under independent baths, equal or not, change nothing at all.
