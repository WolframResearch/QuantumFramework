# Backbone: Feedback Cooling, and the Floor a Measurement Sets

This page is the statement layer of the essay: a graph of assumptions, choices, and derived statements, each given in words and formulas only. Every node names its parents, so the dependency graph is recoverable from the page, and the layers order the reading from the setup to the headline and then to what holds when an assumption is weakened. Three conventions hold throughout. The page has two layers: a construction layer, where the physical roots are translated into the equation S0 by cited standard theory, and an analysis layer, where every consequence consumes S0 as a contract and therefore survives any construction that yields the same equation; the equation is never a root here, because one weakening below (collect only a fraction of the record) reaches beneath it. Every assumption must be consumed by at least one statement that is not its own weakening; a root failing that test is not an assumption of the theory but only a direction along which the model can be varied. And every statement's tail separates provenance (derived in place, cited, or conjectured) from verification (exact decision, analytic cross-check, numerical check, or none), naming the clause scope and what the checking route shares with the finding route. Node labels: R is a root, a setup assumption; C is a choice, a meaning we fixed rather than a fact; S is a derived statement, with S0 reserved for the equation of motion (E would name it if posited as a contract without its construction, which this page does not do); H is the headline, the terminal statement; N is a recorded non-assumption; a prime marks the same statement under a weakened root. Nothing here executes; the computations live downstream, in the catalog essay's section *Feedback Cooling of a Mechanical Oscillator* (in *open-system-simulation-catalog.md*, this folder), whose cells solve the covariance and mean bookkeeping directly. We set $\hbar = 1$.

## The setup, and the choice

**R1 (assumption).** The oscillator is harmonic and every generator that touches it is at most quadratic in the quadratures, with the monitored observable linear: the trap $\omega\,\hat b^\dagger\hat b$, the position readout $\hat x$, the feedback force built from $\hat x$, and the bath coupling through $\hat b$ and $\hat b^\dagger$. In words: the arrangement carries no nonlinearity, so a Gaussian state stays Gaussian and its first two moments close on themselves, which is exactly what later splits the analysis into a deterministic covariance and a stochastic mean. The corner that separates R1 from the rest is a nonlinearity (a Kerr term, or the catalog's monitored qubit): the state leaves the Gaussian family and this whole moment bookkeeping fails.

**R2 (assumption).** One observable, the position $\hat x = (\hat b + \hat b^\dagger)/\sqrt2$, is continuously and weakly monitored with diffusive readout at a single strength $k$; measuring it costs the conjugate momentum a back-action kick, the full $+k$ in the momentum-variance equation, present whatever fraction of the record is kept. This fixes what is watched and the disturbance the watching costs.

**R3 (assumption).** The record is incomplete by a fixed amount: a fraction $\eta$ of the measurement's information is collected, so the disturbance stays $k$ while the recorded information is $\eta k$; at $\eta = 1$ the record is complete. This is a completeness claim, a different kind from R2's existence claim: R2 asserts that position is measured and kicks the momentum, R3 asserts how much of the resulting information the detector keeps, and completeness never follows from existence. The model of S1′, which keeps R2 and weakens R3 to $\eta < 1$, separates the two.

**R4 (assumption).** The oscillator is also coupled to a warm Markovian bath: a decay channel $\hat b$ at rate $\gamma(n_T + 1)$ and an excitation channel $\hat b^\dagger$ at rate $\gamma\,n_T$, an unwatched channel whose own stationary state is thermal at occupation $n_T$. This is the heat the loop fights, and its attractor being mixed rather than the ground state is why watching and feedback together settle above the ground state. The corner that isolates R4 is the bath switched off ($\gamma = 0$): the catalog's Kalman tracker, cold conditional squeezing with no heat pouring in.

**R5 (assumption).** A force $f(t) = -G\,\langle\hat p\rangle_c$ proportional to the conditional momentum estimate is applied as the Hamiltonian term $-f(t)\,\hat x$: a velocity-proportional drag on the estimated motion, with a single gain $G$. The load-bearing feature is not the proportionality but the form: the force enters as a Hamiltonian linear in $\hat x$, so it displaces the mean and leaves the covariance untouched. The corner that isolates R5 is $G = 0$, watching with no action, worked below as S2.

**C1 (choice).** Coldness is scored by the unconditional, record-averaged mean occupation $n = \tfrac12\big(\langle\hat x^2\rangle + \langle\hat p^2\rangle\big) - \tfrac12 = \tfrac12\operatorname{tr}(\Sigma + S) - \tfrac12$, the number an outside thermometer reads. The rival is the conditional occupation $n_{\mathrm{cond}} = \tfrac12\operatorname{tr}\Sigma - \tfrac12$, what the filter alone knows given the record; the two differ by the spread of the estimate across records. The choice is load-bearing, not cosmetic: the conditional occupation is blind to the feedback, since $\Sigma$ carries no gain, so an account scoring coldness by it would find the actuator pointless. Score and story travel together. *Derived in place (the occupation read from the second moments; the two numbers differ by $\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}S$). Verified by numerical check, whole node: both numbers computed side by side in the catalog cells and in this session's scratch, disagreeing at every gain; no shared route.*

## The equation, derived from the setup

**S0 (from R1, R2, R3, R4, R5; cited).** Quantum trajectory theory turns the setup into the conditioned stochastic master equation
$$d\rho = -i\big[\omega\hat b^\dagger\hat b - f(t)\,\hat x,\ \rho\big]dt + k\,\mathcal D[\hat x]\rho\,dt + \sqrt{\eta k}\,\mathcal H[\hat x]\rho\,dW + \gamma(n_T + 1)\,\mathcal D[\hat b]\rho\,dt + \gamma\,n_T\,\mathcal D[\hat b^\dagger]\rho\,dt,\qquad f(t) = -G\,\langle\hat p\rangle_c,$$
with $\mathcal D[\hat c]\rho = \hat c\rho\hat c^\dagger - \tfrac12\{\hat c^\dagger\hat c,\rho\}$, the measurement superoperator $\mathcal H[\hat c]\rho = \hat c\rho + \rho\hat c^\dagger - \langle\hat c + \hat c^\dagger\rangle\rho$, and $dW$ the Wiener increment ($dW^2 = dt$). Each root is visible in the equation's shape: R1 is the generator being quadratic in the operators and linear in the readout; R2 is the disturbance $k\,\mathcal D[\hat x]$ and the readout $\sqrt{\eta k}\,\mathcal H[\hat x]$; R3 is $\eta$ sitting on the readout while the disturbance keeps its full $k$; R4 is the two bath dissipators with their thermal weights; R5 is the Hamiltonian term $-f(t)\hat x$ linear in $\hat x$. *Cited: Wiseman and Milburn, Quantum Measurement and Control (Cambridge 2010), feedback chapter; Doherty and Jacobs, Phys. Rev. A 60, 2700 (1999), for the Gaussian conditional and feedback dynamics; the positivity-preserving filter used for the simulation, Rouchon and Ralph (arXiv:1410.5345). Verification: none; the translation is trusted to its sources, and the catalog computes with exactly this equation.*

## Consequences

**S1 (from S0, R1).** By R1 the state is Gaussian, so the equation collapses to two moment equations. The conditional covariance $\Sigma$ obeys a deterministic Riccati equation, the same for every record,
$$\frac{d}{dt}\begin{pmatrix}\Sigma_{\mathrm{xx}}\\ \Sigma_{\mathrm{xp}}\\ \Sigma_{\mathrm{pp}}\end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix}2\Sigma_{\mathrm{xp}} - 4\eta k\,\Sigma_{\mathrm{xx}}^2 - \gamma\Sigma_{\mathrm{xx}} + \gamma(n_T + \tfrac12)\\[2pt] \Sigma_{\mathrm{pp}} - \Sigma_{\mathrm{xx}} - 4\eta k\,\Sigma_{\mathrm{xx}}\Sigma_{\mathrm{xp}} - \gamma\Sigma_{\mathrm{xp}}\\[2pt] k - 2\Sigma_{\mathrm{xp}} - 4\eta k\,\Sigma_{\mathrm{xp}}^2 - \gamma\Sigma_{\mathrm{pp}} + \gamma(n_T + \tfrac12)\end{pmatrix},$$
the back-action $+k$ from R2 heating the momentum, the information gain $-4\eta k(\cdots)$ carrying R3's efficiency, and R4's damping $\gamma$ and thermal drift $\gamma(n_T + \tfrac12)$ pulling toward the bath. The conditional mean $\vec m = (\langle\hat x\rangle, \langle\hat p\rangle)$ obeys a linear stochastic equation driven by the innovation,
$$d\vec m = A_G\,\vec m\,dt + \vec K\,dW,\qquad A_G = \begin{pmatrix}-\gamma/2 & \omega\\ -\omega & -\gamma/2 - G\end{pmatrix},\qquad \vec K = 2\sqrt{\eta k}\begin{pmatrix}\Sigma_{\mathrm{xx}}\\ \Sigma_{\mathrm{xp}}\end{pmatrix},$$
with $A_G$ the closed loop written out, free rotation $\pm\omega$, bath damping $-\gamma/2$, and R5's feedback the lone $-G$ on the momentum row. Averaged over records the mean's own covariance $S$ solves the Lyapunov balance $A_G S + S A_G^{T} + \vec K\vec K^{T} = 0$, so the total covariance splits, $\Sigma_{\mathrm{tot}} = \Sigma + S$, and the occupation of C1 is $n = \tfrac12\operatorname{tr}(\Sigma + S) - \tfrac12$. The one structural fact the rest of the page turns on: the gain $G$ appears only in $A_G$, hence only in $S$; the conditional covariance $\Sigma$ carries no $G$, because R5 is a Hamiltonian linear in $\hat x$, which moves the mean and never the covariance. *Cited for the moment reduction: Doherty and Jacobs, Phys. Rev. A 60, 2700 (1999); the split $\Sigma + S$ and the feedback-independence of $\Sigma$ derived in place. Verified by numerical check, by clause: $\operatorname{tr}\Sigma$ unchanged as the gain is swept; the Lyapunov occupation at $G = 0$ agreeing with the plain unconditional master equation, two routes sharing no code.*

**S2 (from S1).** With the loop open, $G = 0$, the mean is damped only at the bath rate $\gamma/2$ while the fixed noise $\vec K\vec K^{T}$ keeps pumping $S$, so the record-averaged occupation settles above the bath value $n_T$: the meter leaves the oscillator hotter than it found it. The mechanism is R2's back-action with nothing to remove it, measuring the position kicks the momentum and the open loop carries no drain. This doubles as R5's independence proof, since the $G = 0$ occupation is strictly the $G > 0$ one only at the trivial gain. *Derived in place from S1 at $G = 0$. Verified by numerical check, whole node and at the catalog's parameters: the conditional-plus-spread bookkeeping against the plain unconditional master equation, the two settling on one occupation above $n_T$; no shared code.*

## The headline

**H (from S1, S2, C1).** Closing the loop drains the spread $S$, the $-G$ damping in $A_G$ pulling the cloud of conditional means into the origin, and cools the unconditional occupation, but only down to a hard floor: since $\Sigma$ carries no gain, the reachable occupation is bounded below by the conditional occupation
$$n \ \ge\ n_{\mathrm{cond}} = \tfrac12\operatorname{tr}\Sigma - \tfrac12,$$
which the feedback cannot cross. Mechanism, in words: feedback is a Hamiltonian, so it relocates where the estimate sits without sharpening how well the record knows it; the sharpness is bought only by watching, so the ultimate cold is the conditional covariance the measurement itself fixes, approached but reached only in the ideal limit of a noiseless actuator acting on a perfectly known state. Watching alone heats; the same watching, fed back, cools to exactly the floor that watching set, so the cold a measurement-and-feedback loop can reach is a property of the measurement, not of the actuator. Audit: H rests on the contract of S1 (the Riccati for $\Sigma$ and the linear stochastic equation for $\vec m$) together with C1; the contract is realized from R1 through R5 by the cited translation, so the analysis survives any construction yielding the same moment equations. No conjecture anywhere in the closure. *Derived in place from S1 and S2. Verified by numerical check, whole node and at the catalog's parameters: the closed-loop occupation settling strictly above $n_{\mathrm{cond}}$, computed on a route independent of the covariance derivation.*

## When an assumption is weakened

**H′ (from R5's gain swept; H).** The occupation does not fall monotonically to the floor as the gain grows: it has an interior minimum. Too little gain leaves the bath and back-action heating of S2 uncancelled; too much feeds the record's own shot noise back onto the oscillator as force, because the fed-back estimate $\langle\hat p\rangle_c$ carries the measurement noise $\vec K\,dW$, and a large gain amplifies it. The floor $n_{\mathrm{cond}}$ is untouched across the sweep: the gain optimizes only how close $S$ is pulled to zero, never $\Sigma$. This is the imprecision-back-action tradeoff, between the shot-noise blur on the record and the momentum kick reading the position costs, that caps every measurement-based cooling experiment. *Derived in place from the Lyapunov balance ($\vec K\vec K^{T}$ fixed by the measurement, the damping in $A_G$ growing with $G$). Verified by numerical check at the catalog's single parameter set, the occupation falling then rising across the gain sweep with an interior minimum; other parameters untested.*

**S1′ (from R3 weakened; S0).** Collect only a fraction $\eta < 1$ and trajectory theory changes the equation beneath: the readout coefficient becomes $\sqrt{\eta k}$ while the disturbance $k\,\mathcal D[\hat x]$ keeps its full $k$, so the information gain in the Riccati weakens to $4\eta k$ against a back-action that does not, the conditional covariance $\Sigma$ rises, and with it the floor $n_{\mathrm{cond}}$ and the reachable cold. The floor is set by the completeness of the record, not by the actuator: the same lesson as the catalog's rapid-purification section, where $\eta < 1$ imposed an impurity floor $(1 - \eta)/2$, now imposing a phonon-number floor. This is the weakening that reaches beneath the equation, a lost record share, and is why the page owes its construction layer rather than positing the contract alone. *Derived in place (the Riccati's information gain scaling with $\eta$ against the fixed back-action). Verified by numerical check: $n_{\mathrm{cond}}(\eta)$ and the fixed-gain cold both falling as $\eta \to 1$ across the catalog's efficiency sweep and this session's grid; the strict monotonicity read off a grid, not proved for all $\eta$.*

**N (a recorded non-assumption).** Nothing on this page rests on the oscillator beginning in thermal equilibrium, $\Sigma = (n_T + \tfrac12)\mathbb 1$. The floor, the optimal gain, and the heating are properties of the fixed points of the Riccati and the Lyapunov balance, which the initial covariance never enters; the catalog starts thermal only to make the open-loop and closed-loop transients comparable. The boundary of the deletion: a start already below the conditional floor is not a separate assumption but a different question, the transient down to the floor, and not the steady cold H reports. *Derived in place (the steady occupation is a start-independent fixed point). Verified by numerical check: the late-time occupation independent of the initial covariance in the catalog run.*

## The abstract, read off the graph

A harmonic oscillator has its position watched continuously through a diffusive detector, is pushed by a Hamiltonian force proportional to its own estimated momentum, and all the while exchanges quanta with a warm bath. Because every generator is linear or quadratic and the readout is linear, the state stays Gaussian and its moments close: the conditional covariance follows a deterministic Riccati equation, and the conditional mean follows a linear stochastic equation whose spread across records obeys a Lyapunov balance, so the record-averaged occupation is the conditional covariance plus that spread. Watching alone heats, because measuring position kicks the momentum and the open loop removes nothing. Closing the loop drains the spread and cools, but only the record-averaged occupation, and only down to a floor equal to the conditional occupation the measurement itself fixes: feedback is a Hamiltonian, so it moves where the estimate sits without ever sharpening it, and the cold a loop can reach is therefore set by the measurement, not the actuator. Pushing harder does not help past an optimal gain, where the record's own shot noise, fed back as force, starts to reheat the oscillator; and a leaky detector raises the floor itself, keeping the full back-action while discarding part of the record, exactly the phonon-number counterpart of the impurity floor a lossy measurement leaves in rapid purification. Only the completeness of the record and the presence of the bath are essential to where the cold lands; the thermal start is not, since the floor is an attractor the initial state is forgotten into.
