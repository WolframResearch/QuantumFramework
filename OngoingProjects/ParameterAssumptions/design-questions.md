# Design questions for steps 5 to 8 (2026-11-08)

Each question names the decision its answer changes. An answer counts only after a `wolframscript` check on QF's own outputs: the expressions in `../SymbolicForQuantum/bench/cases.wl` and `cases_hard.wl`, and the cases in `bench.wls` here.

1. **One normal form for trig.** Does `TrigToExp` followed by `Together` give the same rational function in `E^(I θ)` for two equal trig polynomials, including half angles (`θ/2` in rotations)? Does the result grow or shrink on circuit matrices, and does `ExpToTrig` bring it back to a short form? Decides: the exact zero test of step 5 and which direction the trig stage rewrites.
2. **Common denominator cost.** On circuit-matrix entries, when does `Together` grow the expression or take long, and does `Cancel`, or treating numerator and denominator separately, avoid it? Decides: the rational stage of step 5 and its size limit.
3. **Trig reduction cost.** What do `TrigReduce` and `TrigExpand` cost on the same entries, and which gives the shorter form? Decides: whether the trig stage uses one of them or neither.
4. **Matrix tests on symbols.** What do `UnitaryMatrixQ`, `HermitianMatrixQ` and `PositiveSemidefiniteMatrixQ` return for symbolic entries, and do their `SameTest` or `Tolerance` options let QF pass its own zero test? Decides: how the yes/no/undecided properties are built.
5. **Exponentials of rotations.** Does `MatrixExp[-I θ/2 σ]` (and `MatrixFunction` on the same input) return the short cosine-sine form directly for real θ, and at what cost? Decides: whether the construction-time `FullSimplify` of named rotations and evolution can go (step 6).
6. **Sign questions under ranges.** Can `FunctionSign` or `FunctionRange`, given the facts as conditions, decide the sign of `1 - p`, `p (1 - p)`, `Sqrt[1 - p]`, `Cos[θ]^2 - 1/2` faster or more often than `Refine`? Decides: the sign provers of step 5.
7. **Checking a substituted value.** What is the cheapest exact way to decide that a value satisfies, violates, or cannot be checked against a symbol's domain, including inexact values and values that are themselves symbolic? Decides: step 7.
8. **Matrices declared unitary.** Can symbolic arrays declared unitary or Hermitian prove `U^† U = 1` or simplify products without forming entries, and what does structured `Dot` cost on QF's own arrays? Decides: whether keeping circuits in structured form becomes a later step.

## Answers (2026-11-08, each checked with `wolframscript`; scripts and outputs in `probes/`)

1. **Trig normal form: yes for zero, no for display.** After the real symbols are made real and the sign facts applied, `Together[TrigToExp[e]]` has numerator 0 for all ten identities in both case sets, including the 167,000-leaf three-qubit unitarity check (0.65 s, where `FullSimplify` times out). All 18 small expressions reach the same exponential form as their `FullSimplify` result. Half angles need nothing special. The form is often longer than the input, and a nonzero form is not unique, so it serves zero tests only. `Together` without `Trig -> True` treats `Sin` and `Cos` as opaque symbols, so `TrigToExp` must come first. Making symbols real with `ComplexExpand` plus `Refine` cost 1.06 s on the large case, more than the test: the private real copies should do that step instead (`q1zero.wls`, `q12hard.wls`).
2. **Common denominator.** Expanding without `Together` is not faster on large cases (0.98 s against 0.65 s), so the zero test keeps `Together`. `Cancel` never combines the terms of a sum and cannot serve as a zero test.
3. **Trig reduction.** Not needed by the zero test. `TrigReduce` and `TrigExpand` stay candidates only for the display form, behind a size limit.
4. **Matrix tests.** `UnitaryMatrixQ`, `HermitianMatrixQ` and `PositiveSemidefiniteMatrixQ` return False for symbolic entries, ignore `$Assumptions`, and never return "undecided". With QF's zero test as `SameTest`, the unitary and Hermitian tests answer True correctly; the definiteness tests take no such option, so QF needs its own (`q45.wls`).
5. **Exponentials of rotations.** `MatrixExp` of a Pauli rotation or Pauli string returns the short cosine-sine form directly, and `FullSimplify` shortens nothing (RX, RZ, XX, XX+YY). A generator with two noncommuting terms (the Rabi case) comes back about twice the size of its simplified form. `MatrixFunction` gives the same result. Both are correct for complex angles (5 generators, 30 digits; `q5complex.wls`).
6. **Signs under ranges.** Interval arithmetic: about 0.05 ms per expression. `Refine` inside one `$Assumptions` block: 0.2 to 3 ms, and it decides all six test expressions. `FunctionSign`: 1 to 41 ms, with no time limit by default. Order: intervals, then `Refine`; not `FunctionSign` (`q6sign.wls`).
7. **Checking a substituted value.** An exact number: exact comparison with the interval ends. An inexact number: a tolerance band, inside means allowed, outside means violated, within the band means undecided. A symbolic value: its own interval from its symbols' facts, inside, disjoint, or overlapping.
8. **Matrices declared unitary.** A matrix symbol cannot be declared unitary (`MatrixSymbol::symm`). `UnitaryMatrix` refuses a symbolic-angle gate. `ArraySimplify` proves `Inverse[a ⊗ b] . (a ⊗ b) = 1` but leaves `a^† . a` unchanged. Structured `Dot` forms dense products, at 2 to 30 times the cost of a packed product. Keeping circuits structured would need QF's own list of Kronecker factors or blocks, checked factor by factor (`q8arrays.wls`).
