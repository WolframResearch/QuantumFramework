# Declared parameters are real: design note

## The rule

A symbol's role fixes its domain. A parameter declared with `"Parameters"` is real; every other symbol may be complex. The domain belongs to the object, and every quantity that the domain keeps fixed is carried through operations rather than recomputed from the amplitudes.

This is the design `QuantumInterferometer` already uses (photon numbers are nonnegative integers, angles are real, transmissivities lie in [0, 1]), and the one WL uses for symbolic distributions: `DistributionParameterAssumptions[NormalDistribution[μ, σ]]` is `μ ∈ Reals && σ > 0`, and `Expectation` answers from it without conditions.

## What the prototype showed

The prototype (uncommitted, `QuantumState/Properties.m` plus 19 tests in `Tests/QuantumState.wlt`) gets the results right: RZ[θ] on {α, β} gives `Sqrt[|α|^2 + |β|^2]`, an entangling circuit gives one compact |amplitude|^2 per outcome over that norm, the Bloch state gives `{Cos[θ/2]^2, Sin[θ/2]^2}`, and nothing changes when no parameter is declared. The full suite passes.

Its cost exposes the design gap. Every "Norm" and every probability rebuilds ⟨v|v⟩ by expanding the amplitudes: about 1 s for a 3-qubit circuit with 5 angles and 8 symbolic amplitudes, 2.4 s at 4 qubits. The answer was known before the circuit ran: rotations with real angles are unitary, so the output norm is the input norm.

## Design

1. **One domain, on the basis.** `"ParameterAssumptions"` is a property of `QuantumBasis`, beside `"ParameterSpec"`, so states, operators, channels, circuits and measurements read the same facts. It is `Element[params, Reals]` only; the numeric range stored in `"ParameterSpec"` (default `{θ, 0, 1}`) is a plotting range, not a domain.
2. **One helper for simplification on the domain.** Every property that simplifies goes through it. Today three code paths each say "parameters are real" in their own way: `realForm` in `QuantumEntanglement.m`, the symbolic KAK path in `QuantumOperator/Properties.m`, and the prototype. They become one.
3. **Unitarity on the domain, asked once per operator.** An operator answers whether it is unitary for real values of its parameters. Named rotations answer from their name; any other operator by a symbolic check of `U^† U` under the domain.
4. **The norm is carried.** Applying an operator unitary on the domain passes the input's norm to the output. Otherwise, or when unitarity is unknown, the norm is computed from the amplitudes as the prototype does now.
5. **One formula for probabilities.** Pure states: |amplitude|^2 over the norm squared. Mixed states: the diagonal over the trace. The norm comes from the carried value when there is one.
6. **Gate angles are real by role.** A symbol in the angle slot of a named gate joins the real domain, so `QuantumOperator["RZ"[θ]]` gets the real-θ results without `"Parameters" -> {θ}`.

## Decisions (2026-11-07)

- **The norm lives in the state's data.** Every construction path either keeps it or drops it.
- **The property cache is left alone.** Parametric objects keep skipping it (`4c73bd40`). The carried facts (norm, unitarity on the domain) live in the objects themselves, so this design needs no cache for speed or for correctness.
- **Gate angles are real without becoming parameters.** The domain is a list of real symbols on the basis, separate from `"ParameterSpec"`: declared parameters join it, and so does every symbol in the angle slot of a named gate. Substitution (`op[value]`), `"ParameterArity"`, display and caching stay as they are; only the domain grows. `"ParameterAssumptions"` reads the domain list.

## Order of work

1. Real-symbol domain on the basis, merged on composition the way `"ParameterSpec"` is; named gates add their angle symbols.
2. The single helper reads that domain; fold `realForm` and the KAK path into it, with their tests unchanged. Done: `simplifyOnReals[expr, reals]` (Utilities.m) is the one place that simplifies with given symbols real and the rest complex. The state's norm and probabilities pass the basis domain; entanglement, KAK and the stabilizer phase check pass every symbol of their input, which is their documented contract (`QuantumEntangledQ::realparameters`). Open question: whether those three should read the object's domain instead, which would need named states such as `"Werner"[p]` to put their parameter slots in the domain the way named gates do.
3. Unitarity on the domain for operators, then the carried norm through both application paths (Schrodinger and tensor network).
4. Probabilities from the carried norm; retire the prototype's recomputation where a carried value exists.
5. Each step: exact-value tests that fail on the old code, `/wl-verify` and `/wl-quality` on request, the full suite, then commit on approval.

## What other systems do (search, 2026-11-07)

Two designs work, and one does not.

- **The fact lives on the symbol and is used while the expression is built.** SymPy (`Symbol('x', real=True)`), Sage (`var('x', domain='real')`) and Julia Symbolics (the type `Num <: Real`) all make `conjugate(x)` return `x` at once, so the dagger is a transpose plus an elementwise conjugate that is already clean. The price in SymPy: a real `x` and a plain `x` are different objects, so substituting one for the other silently drops the fact.
- **Named quantum gates carry their own inverse rule.** Qiskit, Cirq, PennyLane, Yao and qutip-qip all define `RZ(θ)† = RZ(-θ)`, `U(θ, φ, λ)† = U(-θ, -λ, -φ)`, and conjugate a matrix only for gates given as raw matrices. The rule is right only for real θ, and none of them knows whether θ is real: Cirq and Yao give a silently wrong adjoint for a complex symbol, and Qiskit catches it only when a complex number is bound.
- **Facts kept in a global context reach only the operations written to consult them.** SymPy's `refine` has no rule for `conjugate`; Maple's `assume` works through `is`. WL is the same: `ConjugateTranspose` ignores `$Assumptions` (checked on 15.0.1), while `Refine[..., Element[t, Reals]]` and the plain rule `Conjugate[t] -> t` both give the right `RZ[t]†`, the rule about four times faster. The plain rule does not reach `Conjugate[Cos[t]]`, and must not turn `Conjugate[Sqrt[t]]` into `Sqrt[t]` unless t >= 0, which `Refine` gets right.

What this means for QF:

1. Keep the facts on the object, as the basis record does: WL symbols carry no type, and an object-level record avoids SymPy's problem, since θ stays the same symbol.
2. Use them at every conjugation: one function that conjugates and then replaces `Conjugate[s]` by `s` for real s, with `Refine` under the domain for a `Conjugate` of a function of real symbols only (Cos, Sqrt). Every dagger, bra, density matrix and ⟨v|v⟩ goes through it. (Replaced by the step 4 design below: private real copies of the symbols instead of a rule applied afterwards.)
3. Named gates also invert by their rule (`"RZ"[θ]` dagger is `"RZ"[-θ]`), but only when their angles are in the real domain, which QF knows and the other toolkits do not. Otherwise QF conjugates the matrix.
4. Check the facts when values are substituted: a complex value for a real symbol gives a message, as Qiskit does at binding.

## Step 4 design: facts applied through private copies of the symbols (2026-11-07)

During a computation QF swaps the user's symbols for private copies that carry the facts in the record, works on the copies, and swaps the names back, so nothing it assumes reaches the user's symbols.

1. **The record holds conditions per symbol.** Real, nonnegative, in [0, 1], nonnegative integer, or complex (the user's override), kept by kind, with each symbol's inequalities attached to it. The role sets the default: declared parameters and the angle slots of named gates are real; channel probabilities and the Werner p lie in [0, 1]; Lindblad rates are nonnegative; every other symbol has no fact. A fact names a symbol, plain or indexed (`θ`, `θ[1]`, `Subscript[θ, 1]`), never a pattern. Pattern keys were tried and dropped (2026-11-08): QF stores each computed property as a rule, so a pattern inside an object makes that rule match other objects, and `Refine` reads a pattern only in an `Element` fact, never in an inequality. A later shorthand may expand a pattern into the indexed symbols an object actually holds, so the record itself never holds one. A user declaration wins over a role default; a default that contradicts a stated condition is detected once, when the record is built, and dropped.
2. **The record holds facts about symbols only.** Whether `f[x]` is real or has a sign is worked out from the facts about `x` and a rule per function (a power is real for a real base and integer exponent, or a positive base and real exponent), never stored.
3. **Conjugation runs on private copies.** Any computation that conjugates (dagger, bra, density matrix, inner product, Abs, probabilities) swaps each real symbol for a private symbol carrying `Conjugate[s] = s`, `Re[s] = s`, `Im[s] = 0`, evaluates, swaps the names back and removes the private symbols. The kernel's own evaluation then cleans every conjugation, including `Conjugate[Cos[θ]]` and `Abs[a E^(I θ)]`, which a rule applied afterwards does not reach. Checked on 15.0.1 with the 3-qubit layered circuit matrix: `ConjugateTranspose` leaves 384 `Conjugate` terms with the user's symbols and none with private copies, `U^†U` simplifies to the identity in about 9 ms, and the user's symbols carry no rules afterwards. The bare `ConjugateTranspose` takes about 1 ms either way, so the 629 ms of the old `"Dagger"` on the same circuit is spent elsewhere in QF and is a separate target.
4. **Sign facts go through `Refine`, set up once per operation.** Private copies do not clean `Conjugate[Sqrt[1 - p]]`; `Refine` under `0 <= p <= 1` gives `1 - p`. `Refine[e, facts]` prepares its facts from scratch on every call, while `Refine[e]` inside `Block[{$Assumptions = facts}, ...]` reuses them: 300 small expressions took 86 ms the first way and 2.5 ms the second, with the same results (15.0.1). So each QF operation opens one such block, holding only the facts about the symbols present in its input, and calls `Refine` only on terms that still hold `Conjugate`, `Abs`, `Sqrt` or `Log` of a symbol in the record. Inequalities are the costly kind of fact; "real" stays with the private copies, which cost almost nothing.
5. **No facts, no cost.** An object with an empty record, or with no symbols, skips all of this after one test of the record. This removes the slowdown of `"ProbabilitiesList"` on the current branch.
6. **Yes/no properties have three answers.** `"UnitaryQ"`, `"HermitianQ"`, `"TracePreservingQ"` and `"PhysicalQ"` return True, False, or `Undefined` when undecided, never False for undecided; a time-out counts as undecided. Order: the exact check on private copies first. Then a generic point: distinct transcendental values that satisfy the record, away from its boundaries (an interior value for [0, 1], never 0 or 1). A failure at a valid point, computed exactly, proves False; a pass there is only likely, so it gives `Undefined` unless the exact check said True. `PossibleZeroQ` already tests at sample values and can serve for the second stage once the symbols are private real copies.
7. **A missing sign fact gives a conditional answer, not a refusal.** When a formula needs a sign the record lacks (an unrecorded p under `Sqrt`), QF takes the generic case, returns the result with the condition it used attached ("valid for 0 <= p <= 1"), and warns. This joins step 7, the substitution warning.
8. **Every `Simplify` in these paths has a time limit.** On timeout the correct unsimplified expression is returned, not `$Failed`.
9. **Tests check which stage answered,** not only the result. In a chain of fallbacks a broken cheap stage is invisible, because a slower stage still answers; only a check on the stage catches the slip.
10. **Private symbols never escape.** Names are swapped back on every exit, including an abort, and no stored or cached property may hold a private symbol; a test asserts that no private symbol appears in any output.

The six places that call `ComplexExpand` with every symbol taken as real can be fixed now, apart from the rest: `ComplexExpand[expr, complexSymbols]` takes the listed symbols as complex and all others as real, so `ComplexExpand[Abs[a E^(I t)]^2, {a}]` gives `Re[a]^2 + Im[a]^2`, where the form used now gives `a^2` (15.0.1).

## Step 5 plan: how the facts are applied (2026-11-08)

Additions to the step 4 design, for the function that applies the record.

1. **Each domain is a kind plus two bounds.** Reals, NonNegativeReals, PositiveReals and `Interval[{a, b}]` are all stored as real with a lower and an upper bound (open or closed); Integers adds an integer flag; Complexes stands alone. Two domains for the same symbol meet by taking the larger lower bound and the smaller upper bound. A lower bound above the upper one is a contradiction, found once when the record is built: one message, and the object is then left unsimplified rather than simplified under false facts. This closes the open gap that disjoint intervals go unnoticed.
2. **Exit before any work.** If the record is empty, or the expression holds none of `Conjugate`, `Re`, `Im`, `Abs`, `Arg`, `Sign`, `Sqrt` or `Log` of a recorded symbol, it is returned as is: no swap, no block, no `Refine`.
3. **Signs are proven cheaply before `Refine`.** Each question ("is e >= 0", "is e real") is asked twice, once for the claim and once for its negation, and the two answers give True, False or undecided. Three cheap provers run first:
   - the built-in `Interval` arithmetic: substitute each bounded symbol by its interval and read the sign off the result. `1 - p`, `p (1 - p)` and `Sqrt[1 - p]` with p in [0, 1] all come out as `Interval[{0, 1}]` (15.0.1). The enclosure is wide, so it proves signs but never disproves them;
   - for expressions linear in each symbol separately (`p q`, `1 - p - q + 2 p q`), the extremes over a box sit at its corners, so evaluating the corners settles the sign exactly;
   - a short rule per head: a sum has a sign only if every term has it, a product's sign is the product of its factors' signs, an even power of a real is nonnegative, `Sin` and `Cos` of a real lie in [-1, 1], `Exp` of a real is positive.
   `Refine` under one `$Assumptions` block runs only on what these leave undecided.
4. **What cannot be proven stays unchanged.** Every `Refine` or `Simplify` call has a time limit; on timeout the input comes back, never a partial result. A simplified form is kept only if its `LeafCount` is no larger. One time budget covers a whole user operation, and nested calls get what is left of it.
5. **A missing fact gives a conditional answer.** When a step needs a sign the record lacks, the result is returned as `ConditionalExpression[result, fact]` with the smallest such fact, attached to the whole object (QF objects are not numeric functions, so the condition does not travel through them by itself).
6. **Zero tests.** A nonzero value at a point inside the domain proves "not zero": angles sampled over a full period, probabilities strictly inside (0, 1), complex symbols off the real axis. A point outside the domain proves nothing. "Zero" is proven only by an exact form; a value that vanishes only numerically is undecided. Rewriting `Cos` and `Sin` of real angles through `E^(I θ)` turns unitarity and Hermiticity checks into rational functions, for which exact checks exist. Sampling uses its own fixed random seed so answers repeat, and each cached answer records which test produced it, so a likely answer is never replayed as a proven one.
7. **Eigensystems.** Try the triangular and block-diagonal cases first (tensor products and direct sums are common). For a Hermitian matrix the number of eigenvectors must equal each eigenvalue's multiplicity; a mismatch means a zero test went wrong and gives undecided. Where eigenvalues coincide only for special parameter values, return those values as the condition under which the result holds.
8. **Facts outlive the symbols.** When a symbol drops out of an expression, its fact stays in the record: a later composition can bring it back.
9. **`ComplexExpand` and the record.** The complex-symbol list of `ComplexExpand` accepts patterns (`ComplexExpand[Conjugate[Subscript[a, 1] x], {Subscript[a, _]}]` gives `x Re[a1] - I x Im[a1]`), which a later pattern shorthand could use. It reads no inequality facts, only a `Positive` value set on a symbol (with `Positive[q] = True`, `Abs[q] + Abs[Sqrt[q]]` gives `q + Sqrt[q]`, against `Sqrt[q^2] + (q^2)^(1/4)` without; 15.0.1). So a private copy of a positive symbol can carry that value, and bounded symbols still need `Refine`.
10. **Simplification is aimed, not open-ended.** Where a full simplification is needed, `FullSimplify` gets a short list of transformations chosen for the expressions QF builds (trig of real angles, square roots of probabilities, rational functions), plus a `TimeConstraint`, instead of its default search over every transformation.
11. **The exact zero test comes first.** An expression is zero when, after the facts are applied (`Refine`), split into real and imaginary parts (`ComplexExpand`) and combined over one denominator with trig rewriting (`Together[..., Trig -> True]`), it is the number 0. `PossibleZeroQ` runs only when that leaves it undecided, and its answer counts as likely, not proven (item 6).
12. **A failed simplification proves nothing.** If `Simplify` or `FullSimplify` times out or returns a form that is not 0, the expression is undecided, never taken as nonzero.
13. **`ComplexExpand` always gets the complex symbols.** Every call passes the list of symbols the record marks Complexes, since without it every symbol is taken as real.

## Revised plan for steps 5 to 8 (2026-11-08)

Based on the answers in `design-questions.md`, each checked in the kernel (`probes/`). Where this section and the step 5 plan above disagree, this section holds.

**User declarations and role defaults: no change.** Step 4 already lets a user's Complexes override every role (test `ComplexOverride`). A user interval meets the role's interval, which equals replacing it whenever the user's range lies inside the role's; the two differ only for a range outside the role's, a probability outside [0, 1], where meeting gives the conflict message. Role ranges are the physical ones, so the message is kept.

**Step 5: one cleanup function.** QF calls it once per user operation, on the whole result.

1. **Matrices are split first.** Rows and columns that never meet through a nonzero entry form separate blocks, and each block is cleaned on its own. One cache, keyed on the expression and the facts used, is shared by every entry of the call and dropped when the call ends, so a repeated piece is cleaned once.
2. **A fixed sequence, cheapest first, with no search.** A scan of the heads present decides which stages run at all. Each stage has a size limit and a time limit, and its result is kept only if it is no larger than its input.
   1. Private real copies of the real symbols (step 4), which clean every `Conjugate`, `Re`, `Im` and `Abs` of them during evaluation. This replaces `ComplexExpand` plus `Refine` for making symbols real, which was the most expensive part of the zero test on large expressions.
   2. Sign-aware rules for `Conjugate`, `Abs` and `Sqrt` of functions of recorded symbols. A sign is asked as yes, no or unknown: interval arithmetic first (each bounded symbol replaced by its interval), then `Refine` inside one `$Assumptions` block for what the intervals leave open. `FunctionSign` is not used: slower, and without a time limit by default. An `Abs` stays until its argument's sign is proven: `Sqrt[p^2]` becomes `Abs[p]`, and `Abs[p]` becomes `p` only for p known nonnegative.
   3. A common denominator, with numerator and denominator cleaned separately.
   4. `FullSimplify` only on pieces below a size limit, with a time limit and a short list of transformations.
3. **A timeout returns the best form found so far for each piece.** Every intermediate form equals the input, so this is always correct and never worse than the input.
4. **Exact zero test.** After stage 1 and the sign facts, an expression is zero when `Together[TrigToExp[e]]` has numerator 0. `TrigToExp` must come first: `Together` alone treats `Sin` and `Cos` as opaque symbols. The test proved every identity in both case sets, including the 167,000-leaf three-qubit unitarity check in 0.65 s. The exponential form is used only for this test, never as a displayed result: it is often longer, and a nonzero form is not unique. `PossibleZeroQ` runs only where this test does not apply (a function other than trig, exponential, power or rational), and its answer counts as likely, not proven.
5. **Yes/no/undecided matrix properties are QF's own.** The built-in matrix tests answer False for any symbolic entry, ignore assumptions and never answer "undecided". `"UnitaryQ"` and `"HermitianQ"` use the zero test above on the entries of `U^† U - 1` and `H - H^†`, after stage 1. `"PhysicalQ"` and positivity use the sign questions of stage 2 on the eigenvalues or principal minors, and answer undecided when a sign is unknown.

**Step 6: remove unasked simplification.** Every `Simplify` or `FullSimplify` nobody asked for becomes a call to the step 5 function without its last stage. Named rotations and Pauli-string exponentials lose their construction-time `FullSimplify` outright: `MatrixExp` already returns their short form, and `FullSimplify` shortened none of them. Exponentials of a sum of noncommuting terms (the Rabi case) come back about twice the size of their simplified form; they go through the step 5 function, not `FullSimplify`.

**Step 7: check substituted values.** When a value replaces a recorded symbol, QF asks whether the record allows it: yes, no or unknown. An exact number is compared exactly with the interval ends. An inexact number uses a tolerance band: inside means allowed, outside means violated, within the band means unknown. A symbolic value gets its own interval from its symbols' facts: inside means allowed, disjoint means violated, overlapping means unknown. Only "no" gives a message. This step rests on reasoning, not yet on a kernel check, and gets its tests when it is built.

**Step 8: named-gate inverse rule.** Unchanged: `"RZ"[θ]` dagger is `"RZ"[-θ]` only when the record says θ is real; otherwise the matrix is conjugated.

**Later, possibly: circuits kept as factors.** A circuit kept as QF's own list of Kronecker factors or blocks can be checked factor by factor: a product of n one-qubit gates becomes n checks of 2 by 2 matrices. The built-in symbolic and structured arrays cannot carry this: a matrix symbol cannot be declared unitary, a gate with a symbolic angle is refused as a unitary structured array, and structured products cost 2 to 30 times a packed product. Decided after steps 5 and 6 are measured.

**Before step 5 starts:** time the cases in `bench.wls` and `../SymbolicForQuantum/bench/` against main, so each stage is kept only if it measurably helps, and the tests check which stage produced each answer.
