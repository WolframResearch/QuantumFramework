# Compete on accuracy where incumbents chase throughput

The Wolfram Quantum Framework (QF) should lead its numerical studies with four problem classes that incumbents already benchmark but do not certify: the **driven-dissipative Kerr oscillator**, the **multilevel transmon under pulsed drive**, **small error-correcting codes under non-Pauli noise**, and **cavity-QED spectra**. Across the thirteen framework families surveyed, almost every cross-library comparison of open-system, bosonic or stabilizer tools is written by the developers of the winning package and times a fixed configuration; none measures error against a converged reference ([QuantumToolbox.jl paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.21440); [HierarchicalEOM.jl paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.07522)). Their issue trackers meanwhile record the same accuracy failures year after year: adaptive integrators that skip pulses, Fock-space truncation left to the user, steady-state solvers that return wrong answers without warning, and floating-point arithmetic that misreports an exponentially small splitting by ten orders of magnitude ([QuTiP #2814](https://github.com/qutip/qutip/issues/2814); [scqubits #274](https://github.com/scqubits/scqubits/issues/274); [QuTiP #3026](https://github.com/qutip/qutip/issues/3026); [QuantumToolbox.jl precision guide](https://qutip.org/QuantumToolbox.jl/stable/users_guide/arbitrary_precision)). Retirements in 2025–26 widen the opening: Qiskit removed Pulse and archived Dynamics, PennyLane's development branch deletes its pulse, noise-model, qudit and continuous-variable code, and Xanadu deprecated Strawberry Fields and archived The Walrus and MrMustard, so pulse-level multilevel dynamics and Fock-space photonics have lost their maintained reference material. Each QF study should pair a *match leg*, which reproduces an incumbent's published number on that incumbent's own reference problem with versions, precision and tolerances pinned, with an *extend leg* into the regime where the same incumbent documents failure. Throughput frontiers (Stim at large code distance, Gaussian boson sampling, Sycamore contraction, GPU-scale Lindblad simulation) are unwinnable and irrelevant to a master's audience. The ranking is inference from cited evidence; no source measured what master's students find compelling.

## Incumbents race on seconds while their users report wrong answers

Every fact below links to a primary source (paper, repository file, documentation page or issue thread) read on 2026-10-04. Sentences marked *Inference* are this report's synthesis, not claims a source makes, and facts that came through a summarising fetch or a search snippet are flagged where used. Nothing here assesses QF itself; every bar is stated in terms of what the incumbents do.

The only recent benchmark that runs QuTiP, QuantumOptics.jl, QuantumToolbox.jl and Dynamiqs side by side was published by QuantumToolbox.jl's authors, who overlap with the QuTiP team, and it concludes that "QuantumToolbox.jl outperforms other packages in all scenarios" ([QuantumToolbox.jl paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.21440)). Its driven Kerr oscillator at Fock cutoff N = 50 is the closest thing the open-system field has to a shared yardstick.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Master equation (`mesolve`) | **0.026 s** | 0.045 s | 0.046 s | 0.206 s |
| Monte Carlo, 100 trajectories | 0.018 s | 0.023 s | 6.8 s (fixed-step jump SSE) | 0.129 s |
| Stochastic master equation, 100 trajectories | 1.0 s | 4.4 s | 11.4 s | 6.8 s |
| Reverse-mode gradient, N = 100 | 12.8 s | n/a | 13.1–13.5 s | 16.7–17.3 s |

Medians converted from the published result files ([benchmark data](https://github.com/albertomercurio/QuantumToolbox.jl-Paper-Figures/tree/main/src/benchmarks)); hardware was an Intel i9-13900KF and an NVIDIA RTX 4090, with package versions as of 28 August 2025 ([QuantumToolbox.jl paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.21440)).

The table measures seconds, not error, and it mixes methods. Dynamiqs' "mcsolve" entry is a jump stochastic Schrödinger equation with a fixed `EulerJump(dt=1e-3)` step rather than adaptive Monte Carlo ([Dynamiqs benchmark script](https://github.com/albertomercurio/QuantumToolbox.jl-Paper-Figures/blob/main/src/benchmarks/python/dynamiqs_benchmarks.py)), and a QuTiP issue reporting `smesolve` "more than 10x slower" in 5.2 used this very script as its reproducer ([QuTiP #2733](https://github.com/qutip/qutip/issues/2733)). The pattern repeats elsewhere: HierarchicalEOM.jl benchmarks itself against QuTiP-BoFiN, built by an overlapping team, and Piquasso benchmarks itself against Strawberry Fields ([HierarchicalEOM.jl](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.07522); [Piquasso](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.04006v3)). Independent studies exist only for gate-model state-vector simulators, where Jamadagni, Läuchli and Hempel found run times differing by **more than two orders of magnitude**, Qiskit and PennyLane running double precision despite documented single-precision support, and PennyLane returning inconsistent random-circuit results on GPU ([Jamadagni et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.09076)). The only accuracy-flavoured metrics in any incumbent benchmark are Piquasso's probability loss versus cutoff and Dynamiqs' count of rejected integrator steps at drive discontinuities ([Piquasso](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.04006v3); [Dynamiqs benchmarks](https://github.com/dynamiqs/dynamiqs/blob/main/benchmarks/README.md)).

Issue trackers and forums record the accuracy story the benchmarks omit, and four failure modes recur across packages. **Pulses vanish inside adaptive steps.** QuTiP's own documentation shows a variable-step solver missing a pulse on t ∈ [0.7, 0.75] and advises a `max_step` below half the pulse width ([QuTiP docs](https://qutip.readthedocs.io/en/latest/guide/dynamics/dynamics-time.html)); at least six people have reported the symptom since 2020, including a π/2–wait–π/2 sequence that returns an excited population of 0.5 instead of 1 once the wait exceeds about 23 time units, and an open 2026 request for automatic pulse detection ([#1265](https://github.com/qutip/qutip/issues/1265); [#1945](https://github.com/qutip/qutip/issues/1945); [#2552](https://github.com/qutip/qutip/issues/2552); [#2814](https://github.com/qutip/qutip/issues/2814)). Dynamiqs makes users declare discontinuity times for modulated drives, and its adaptive Rouchon3 method crashed on discontinuous Hamiltonians in 2026 ([Dynamiqs performance guide](https://www.dynamiqs.org/stable/documentation/basics/performance.html); [Dynamiqs #1085](https://github.com/dynamiqs/dynamiqs/issues/1085)). **Truncation is the user's problem.** A scqubits user found the default charge cutoff "too small for devices with EJ > EC … it took me about 8 months to realize this was a problem" ([scqubits #274](https://github.com/scqubits/scqubits/issues/274)). The tools also disagree on what truncation means: Strawberry Fields computes exact matrix elements and then truncates, so traces fall below 1, while QuTiP and QuantumOptics.jl exponentiate the truncated generator, so states stay normalised but amplitudes near the cutoff are wrong ([Xanadu forum](https://discuss.pennylane.ai/t/fock-backend-truncation/3545); [QuTiP states.py](https://github.com/qutip/qutip/blob/master/qutip/core/states.py); [QuantumOpticsBase fock.jl](https://github.com/qojulia/QuantumOpticsBase.jl/blob/master/src/fock.jl)). Only QuantumToolbox.jl adjusts the cutoff automatically during evolution ([QuantumToolbox.jl paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.21440)). **Steady states fail silently.** QuTiP leaves multiple steady states to the user ("the onus is on the user"), and an open September 2026 issue reports that its eigenvalue method "silently returns a completely wrong, non-physical state" for a Jaynes–Cummings model at N = 4 ([QuTiP 5 paper §3.2.6](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.04705); [QuTiP #3026](https://github.com/qutip/qutip/issues/3026)). QuantumOptics.jl's iterative solver gives seed-dependent answers for a quartic oscillator, and Dynamiqs has no steady-state solver at all ([QuantumOptics.jl #428](https://github.com/qojulia/QuantumOptics.jl/issues/428); [Dynamiqs API](https://github.com/dynamiqs/dynamiqs/blob/main/docs/python_api/index.md)). **Floating point decides the physics.** For a quartic double well, "Float64 reports a splitting of ∼10⁻¹³ while the true value is ∼10⁻²³" ([QuantumToolbox.jl precision guide](https://qutip.org/QuantumToolbox.jl/stable/users_guide/arbitrary_precision)). Dynamiqs and Cirq default to single precision, PennyLane has had four independent "probabilities do not sum to 1" bugs since 2023, and QuTiP returns `fidelity(rho, rho)` = 1.0000000102 for a rank-2 state ([Dynamiqs sharp bits](https://www.dynamiqs.org/stable/documentation/getting_started/sharp-bits.html); [Cirq simulation](https://quantumai.google/cirq/simulate/simulation); [PennyLane #9000](https://github.com/PennyLaneAI/pennylane/issues/9000); [QuTiP #3024](https://github.com/qutip/qutip/issues/3024)).

The ecosystem has also contracted, and the losses concentrate in three niches: pulse-level and time-dependent simulation inside the gate-model stacks, Fock-space photonics, and qudits.

The active core is healthy by contrast: QuTiP 5.3.1, QuantumToolbox.jl 0.50.0, Dynamiqs 0.3.6, QuantumOptics.jl 1.2.10, Stim 1.16.0, quimb 1.15.0, TensorCircuit-NG 1.10.0, Piquasso 8.0.1, Pulser 1.9.1, Cirq 1.7.0 and OpenFermion 1.8.1 all shipped releases in 2026 (sources in the tables of the next section).

*Inference:* the axis left open to a newcomer is accuracy at a stated cost on teaching-scale problems, where every incumbent already finishes in seconds and raw speed decides nothing, together with maintained worked material in the orphaned niches. The audience's reference point matters as well. The research found recent master's-level quantum-optics and open-systems teaching running almost entirely on QuTiP, from Trinity College Dublin's MSc worksheets to the courses QuTiP lists on its education page, found no 2022–26 master's quantum-optics course built on Mathematica, and found no syllabus that lists truncation or tolerance convergence as a topic ([QuTiP in education](https://qutip.org/education); [Trinity College Dublin MSc worksheets](https://github.com/Ilumirnau/Open-Quantum-Systems)). These are absence-based findings, but they imply that the studies most likely to persuade are the ones that meet QuTiP's examples on their own ground.

All four libraries teach the same cavity-QED canon (damped Jaynes–Cummings and vacuum Rabi oscillations, the driven Kerr oscillator, cat-state decoherence, homodyne and photon-counting trajectories) and use dissipative Ising chains for scaling. *Inference:* that canon is the natural ground for a recognised head-to-head. Their reach is bounded the same way: full Lindblad simulation of a dissipative Ising chain takes about **10³ s at 10 spins on one CPU and at 12 spins on an RTX 4090** in every library ([QuantumToolbox.jl §6](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.21440)), and beyond that the incumbents switch to trajectories, permutation symmetry, cumulant expansions or low-rank methods ([PIQS](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.05129); [QuantumCumulants.jl](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.01657)). For non-Markovian problems, only QuTiP and QuantumToolbox.jl (through HierarchicalEOM.jl) offer numerically exact HEOM.

## Twelve problem classes ranked for a master's audience

The ranking weighs five things: whether numerics change the qualitative answer rather than a digit, whether an incumbent publishes the reference problem with parameters a QF study can reuse, whether an incumbent documents a weakness, absence or retirement in that class, whether the physics sits inside master's-level quantum mechanics, and whether the answer is reachable at teaching scale. Eight of the twelve classes are already well served, and two of the top four are deliberately among them: for an audience that believes QF is symbolic, the persuasive move is to match an incumbent on its own ground and then go further. Three competition modes recur. *Match* means reproducing an incumbent's published numbers on its reference problem. *Beat on workflow* means reaching the same answer with convergence evidence built in, fewer manual interventions, or one environment instead of glue code across packages. *Beat on capability* means answering a question the incumbents cannot pose, or by their own documentation get wrong. The order, the modes and the bars below are this report's inference from the cited facts.

### Lead studies pair a published benchmark with a documented failure

**1. Driven-dissipative Kerr oscillator: bistability, Liouvillian gap and cat states.** This is the strongest single anchor because it is the only problem with a published, scripted, four-library head-to-head. The model is Ĥ = Δâ†â − Uâ†²â² + F(â + â†) with thermal photon loss, run at Fock cutoff N = 50 with **Δ = 0.1, U = −0.05, F = 2, γ = 1, n_th = 0.2** over t ∈ [0, 10] with 100 save points and 100 trajectories ([QuantumToolbox.jl paper, Eq. 31](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.21440); [benchmark script](https://github.com/albertomercurio/QuantumToolbox.jl-Paper-Figures/blob/main/src/benchmarks/python/qutip_benchmarks.py)). QuTiP teaches the same physics in Lecture 14 and in a notebook where Kerr cats "become coherent" under trajectories, and Dynamiqs features a driven-dissipative Kerr example with the periodic revival of a coherent state ([Lecture 14](https://nbviewer.org/urls/qutip.org/qutip-tutorials/tutorials-v5/lectures/Lecture-14-Kerr-nonlinearities.ipynb); [notebook 018](https://nbviewer.org/urls/qutip.org/qutip-tutorials/tutorials-v5/time-evolution/018_measures-trajectories-cats-kerr.ipynb); [Dynamiqs Kerr example](https://www.dynamiqs.org/stable/documentation/advanced_examples/kerr-oscillator.html)). For a master's student it is the harmonic oscillator plus one nonlinearity plus loss: the route to Yurke–Stoler cat states and to the collapse and revival Kirchmair et al. observed ([Yurke & Stoler](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.57.13); [Kirchmair et al.](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature11902)), and a Trinity College Dublin MSc worksheet already assigns a cavity with a nonlinear medium ([TCD worksheets](https://github.com/Ilumirnau/Open-Quantum-Systems)). Whether the steady state is unique, how slowly the oscillator switches between bistable branches, and whether the cutoff is converged are questions only numerics settle: the Liouvillian gap "becomes exponentially small near a bistability, and shrinks further with system size" ([QuantumToolbox.jl precision guide](https://qutip.org/QuantumToolbox.jl/stable/users_guide/arbitrary_precision)).

The incumbents are strongest here on throughput. Every library finishes the N = 50 master equation in under a quarter of a second, Dynamiqs batches parameter sweeps, and QuantumToolbox.jl grows and shrinks the Fock cutoff during evolution and applies displacement transformations to strongly driven Kerr systems ([QuantumToolbox.jl paper §3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.21440)). They are weakest at the physics that makes the problem interesting. QuTiP leaves multiple steady states to the user, QuantumOptics.jl's iterative solver gives seed-dependent results for a quartic oscillator, Dynamiqs has no steady-state solver, and QuantumToolbox.jl's own guide warns that the gap "computed in Float64 … eventually stops being physics" ([QuTiP 5 paper §3.2.6](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.04705); [QuantumOptics.jl #428](https://github.com/qojulia/QuantumOptics.jl/issues/428); [Dynamiqs API](https://github.com/dynamiqs/dynamiqs/blob/main/docs/python_api/index.md); [precision guide](https://qutip.org/QuantumToolbox.jl/stable/users_guide/arbitrary_precision)). Dynamiqs' benchmark notes add that the Kerr spectrum "grows as n², which bounds the explicit-RK step size", and that the vectorised Liouvillian sets a memory ceiling ([Dynamiqs benchmarks](https://github.com/dynamiqs/dynamiqs/blob/main/benchmarks/README.md)). A periodically driven variant connects to the Floquet benchmarks in QuTiP's paper and Dynamiqs' suite ([QuTiP 5 paper §3.2.10](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.04705)).

To persuade, a QF study must first match: reproduce ⟨â†â⟩(t) for the published parameters at the incumbents' default tolerances (relative 10⁻⁶, absolute 10⁻⁸ in QuTiP, QuantumToolbox.jl and QuantumOptics.jl) and report wall time on stated hardware beside the published 0.026–0.206 s spread, then repeat for the 100-trajectory Monte Carlo and stochastic legs ([QuTiP integrator defaults](https://github.com/qutip/qutip/blob/master/qutip/solver/integrator/scipy_integrator.py); [QuantumToolbox.jl defaults](https://github.com/qutip/QuantumToolbox.jl/blob/main/src/time_evolution/time_evolution.jl); [QuantumOptics.jl defaults](https://github.com/qojulia/QuantumOptics.jl/blob/master/src/timeevolution_base.jl)). It must then beat on capability: sweep the drive F through the bistable region and report the Liouvillian gap and the steady state or states, with convergence shown jointly in Fock cutoff and working precision, including the regime where the gap drops below double-precision resolution. *Inference:* precision without basis convergence would not clear the bar, because QuantumToolbox.jl's own guide shows even BigFloat returning a wrong answer when the basis is too small.

**2. Transmon beyond two levels: leakage, DRAG and decoherence during pulses.** The transmon is a particle in a cosine potential whose "charge dispersion decreases exponentially with E_J/E_C, while its loss in anharmonicity is described by a weak power law" ([Koch et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0703002)), which turns master's-level perturbation theory into a design trade-off. Once the transmon is driven, its third level makes leakage, the DRAG correction and the validity of the rotating-wave approximation questions without a closed form for finite pulses; Motzoi et al. showed that a second quadrature control "proportional to the time derivative of the first" suppresses leakage ([Motzoi et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/0901.0534)). Courses already teach the ingredients with QuTiP: Rabi oscillations, the RWA with detuning, Ramsey sequences and composite pulses in a 2025 UMass course, and the limits of RWA validity at Trinity College Dublin ([UMass ECE 550/650](https://github.com/UMassIonTrappers/Introduction-to-Quantum-Computing); [TCD worksheets](https://github.com/Ilumirnau/Open-Quantum-Systems)).

Statics are superbly served: scqubits computes spectra, charge dispersion, matrix elements and coherence estimates for five qubit families and arbitrary circuits ([scqubits paper](https://quantum-journal.org/papers/q-2021-11-17-583/)). Dynamics are where the incumbents have withdrawn. Qiskit removed Pulse and archived Dynamics, whose two-transmon `DynamicsBackend` at three levels per transmon was the closest maintained reference ([Dynamics tutorial](https://qiskit-community.github.io/qiskit-dynamics/tutorials/dynamics_backend.html)); PennyLane's development branch deletes its pulse module, and its optimal-control demo stated "we do not consider sources of noise in the system, such as leakage, dephasing" ([PR 10238](https://github.com/PennyLaneAI/pennylane/pull/10238); [optimal-control demo](https://pennylane.ai/qml/demos/tutorial_optimal_control)). scqubits hands dynamics to hand-written QuTiP calls, and that bridge is broken for fresh installs ([scqubits #320](https://github.com/scqubits/scqubits/issues/320)); QuTiP itself remains capable but skips thin pulses after idle periods unless the user sets `max_step` ([#1265](https://github.com/qutip/qutip/issues/1265); [#2814](https://github.com/qutip/qutip/issues/2814)). Interest is rising rather than falling: Dynamiqs' 2026 benchmark suite includes a driven transmon with a DRAG-like pulse and a cross-resonance gate, and a 2026 comparison of transmon models recommends circuit-based multilevel models "for high-fidelity reference simulation or detailed leakage analysis" ([Dynamiqs benchmarks](https://github.com/dynamiqs/dynamiqs/blob/main/benchmarks/README.md); [Larssen et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23034)). *Inference:* the research found no maintained incumbent tutorial on transmon DRAG leakage, but the QuTiP and Dynamiqs checks were keyword searches, so this absence is weak evidence.

The reference problems are scqubits' `demo_transmon` at E_J = 30.02 GHz and E_C = 1.2 GHz over n_g ∈ [−2, 2], including the charge dispersion of the 0→1 and 0→2 transitions versus E_J/E_C ([demo_transmon](https://github.com/scqubits/scqubits-examples/blob/master/examples/demo_transmon.ipynb)); the archived Qiskit Dynamics tutorials, whose pulse-optimisation example is a two-level model (ν = 5.0, r = 0.02) with no leakage level ([pulse tutorial](https://github.com/qiskit-community/qiskit-dynamics/blob/main/docs/tutorials/optimizing_pulse_sequence.rst)); and qutip-qoc's Hadamard gate, which all four algorithms bring above 0.99 fidelity ([QuTiP 5 paper §4.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.04705)). A persuasive study matches scqubits' spectra and dispersion curves and shows the charge-basis convergence that cost one scqubits user eight months ([scqubits #274](https://github.com/scqubits/scqubits/issues/274)). It then beats on capability: leakage into the second excited level versus gate duration for Gaussian and DRAG pulses under T1 and T2, checked against Motzoi's prediction, inside Ramsey and echo sequences with long idle gaps that come out right without hand-set step limits. Pulse optimisation with leakage in the cost function is the natural extension, measured against qutip-qoc and the archived Dynamics X gate.

**3. Small surface and repetition codes beyond Pauli noise.** A threshold is a purely numerical object: Stim's tutorial finds it by "trying a bunch of physical error rates and code distances … see where the curves cross" ([Stim getting-started notebook](https://github.com/quantumlib/Stim/blob/main/doc/getting_started.ipynb)). Students who know stabilizers and Kraus operators can watch the answer move with the noise model: the notebook's uniform circuit noise gives a surface-code threshold of "roughly 1%", while the SD6 and SI1000 models give **0.5–0.7% and 0.3–0.5%** ([notebook](https://github.com/quantumlib/Stim/blob/main/doc/getting_started.ipynb); [Gidney et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.10457)). Google's Willow memory anchors the topic in experiment with Λ = 2.14 ± 0.02 ([arXiv:2408.13687](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.13687)), and the NordIQuEst 2024 autumn school teaches it with Stim and PyMatching ([NordIQuEst notebook](https://nordiquest.net/application-library/training-material/qas2024/notebooks/surface_code_threshold.html)).

Stim, sinter and PyMatching are unbeatable on throughput, but Stim's README states its limits plainly: no non-Clifford gates, only Pauli noise channels ("eg. no amplitude decay"), and only single-control Pauli feedback; the Stim paper adds that "relaxation to the ground state and leaking outside the computational basis" are not Pauli channels ([Stim README](https://github.com/quantumlib/Stim); [Stim paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.02202)). Users who need leakage fork Stim, as Riverlane's Deltakit-Stim does with added leakage instructions (read through a summarising fetch), or approximate ([Deltakit-Stim](https://github.com/Deltakit/deltakit-stim)). Generic simulators such as Aer's density-matrix method and Cirq's density-matrix simulator do apply amplitude damping to small circuits ([Aer noise models](https://qiskit.github.io/qiskit-aer/tutorials/3_building_noise_models.html); [Cirq noisy simulation](https://quantumai.google/cirq/simulate/noisy_simulation)). *Inference:* neither builds detector error models or decodes, so the exact-channel version of the threshold question has no integrated incumbent workflow.

The match leg regenerates the notebook's d = 3 and d = 5 rotated surface-code points (identical circuits exported as Stim text, the same p, the same decoder) and lands within binomial error bars at the notebook's shot budgets of up to 10⁶ shots and 5,000 errors per point. The capability leg swaps the Pauli channels for amplitude damping, coherent over-rotation and leakage into a third level, decodes the same way, and reports how far the Pauli-twirled logical error rate sits from the exact one, which is precisely the question a Pauli-frame simulator cannot pose. *Inference (arithmetic):* a d = 3 rotated surface code has 17 qubits, so its density matrix holds 2³⁴ ≈ 1.7×10¹⁰ entries, about 275 GB in complex128. The exact leg must therefore use trajectories on 2¹⁷-amplitude states, or density matrices for repetition codes, whose 9-qubit d = 5 circuit needs only 2¹⁸ entries; a 17-site qutrit state holds 3¹⁷ ≈ 1.3×10⁸ amplitudes, about 2 GB. A Clifford measurement-induced phase transition, which TensorCircuit-NG features through its Stim-backed engine, is a match-only alternative that needs no decoder ([arXiv:2602.14167](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14167)).

**4. Cavity-QED spectra and photon statistics.** Spectra are where the master equation meets the laboratory through the quantum regression theorem, and they are what master's courses compute: the "Numerical Methods for Quantum Optics and Open Quantum Systems" lectures build the Jaynes–Cummings emission spectrum and the Mollow triplet, and Johansson's QuTiP lectures, reportedly written for a Chalmers course, cover correlation functions and the ultrastrong-coupling Jaynes–Cummings model ([lectures](https://daniele-pybectn.github.io/Computational_quantum_optics_lectures/); [qutip-lectures](https://github.com/jrjohansson/qutip-lectures)). This is QuTiP's home ground, with two-time correlation functions, an exponential-series spectrum, an FFT route, and worked examples for vacuum Rabi splitting and for g⁽¹⁾ and g⁽²⁾ of coherent, thermal and Fock light ([QuTiP correlation guide](https://qutip.readthedocs.io/en/latest/guide/guide-correlation.html)). QuantumToolbox.jl adds pseudo-inverse and Lanczos spectrum solvers, QuantumOptics.jl has a `timecorrelations` module, and Dynamiqs has none ([QuantumToolbox.jl API](https://github.com/qutip/QuantumToolbox.jl/blob/main/docs/src/resources/api.md); [QuantumOptics.jl API](https://github.com/qojulia/QuantumOptics.jl/blob/master/docs/src/api.md); [Dynamiqs API](https://github.com/dynamiqs/dynamiqs/blob/main/docs/python_api/index.md)).

The documented weaknesses concern method rather than capacity. In 2021 a user found `correlation_2op_1t()` and `spectrum()` "giving the physically incorrect result unless solver='es'", and the current guide warns that FFT spectra need correlations evaluated "for a sufficient long time and sufficiently high sampling rate" ([QuTiP #1460](https://github.com/qutip/qutip/issues/1460); [correlation guide](https://qutip.readthedocs.io/en/latest/guide/guide-correlation.html)); time-dependent two-time correlations were also once wrong ([QuTiP #1808](https://github.com/qutip/qutip/issues/1808)). The match leg reproduces the guide's vacuum-Rabi-split spectrum and g⁽²⁾ curves and the resonance-fluorescence spectrum of QuTiP's Lecture 13 ([Lecture 13](https://nbviewer.org/urls/qutip.org/qutip-tutorials/tutorials-v5/lectures/Lecture-13-Resonance-flourescence.ipynb)). The workflow leg obtains each spectrum by two independent routes, a pseudo-inverse (resolvent) route like QuantumToolbox.jl's and an FFT of the two-time correlation, and shows them agreeing to a stated tolerance, with the record-length and sampling convergence the guide leaves to the user made explicit. *Inference:* the numerically decisive extension is the ultrastrong-coupling regime in which the rotating-wave approximation fails, a regime QuTiP's lecture series covers and Trinity's MSc worksheets probe, because there the spectrum's structure follows from computation rather than from a textbook formula ([QuTiP gallery](https://qutip.org/qutip-tutorials/); [TCD worksheets](https://github.com/Ilumirnau/Open-Quantum-Systems)).

### Second-wave studies match strong incumbents and win on explanation

**5. Exponentially small splittings and gaps.** Tunnelling through a double-well barrier is a staple of master's quantum mechanics, and master's numerical-methods notes teach the grid solvers such problems need ([Giannozzi lecture notes](https://www.fisica.uniud.it/~giannozz/Corsi/MQ/LectureNotes/mq.pdf)). QuantumToolbox.jl turned it into a precision benchmark in July 2026: for a quartic double well, "Float64 reports a splitting of ∼10⁻¹³ while the true value is ∼10⁻²³ — wrong by nearly ten orders of magnitude", and the Float64 curve "claims that beyond x₀ ≈ 3 the barrier stops mattering", which the guide calls "a qualitatively wrong statement" ([QuantumToolbox.jl precision guide](https://qutip.org/QuantumToolbox.jl/stable/users_guide/arbitrary_precision)). The same page names the Liouvillian gap near bistability as the open-system analogue, which ties this class to the Kerr study. QuantumToolbox.jl is the incumbent that documents arbitrary-precision states, operators and solvers, and it claims that QuTiP's data layer hard-codes double complex and that JAX supports only hardware floats; that is a competitor's claim, not QuTiP's documentation. Its own caveats define the bar: BigFloat costs one to two orders of magnitude in speed, extended precision has no GPU support, `eigsolve` hard-codes a 10⁻⁸ tolerance, `steadystate`, `mcsolve` and `spectrum` "are not currently tested at high precision", and "at N = 150, even BigFloat returns a wrong splitting — the arithmetic is exact but the basis is not" ([precision guide](https://qutip.org/QuantumToolbox.jl/stable/users_guide/arbitrary_precision)). Matching means reproducing the ~10⁻²³ splitting and the Float64 failure on the guide's potential. Beating it means certifying the number: convergence in basis size past the N = 150 failure and in working precision at once, a cross-check by an independent discretisation, and the same treatment for the steady states and spectral gaps the guide leaves untested. *Inference:* frame this study as convergence and verification rather than as extra digits, because an audience that already files QF under symbolic work could read an arbitrary-precision showcase as more of the same.

**6. Rydberg arrays: blockade, adiabatic order and scars.** Rydberg arrays reduce to two-level atoms with a 1/r⁶ interaction, so students can derive the blockade radius R_b = (C₆/Ω)^(1/6) and the √N enhancement of the collective Rabi frequency, then need numerics for gap closing during an adiabatic sweep, finite-size Z2 order and scar revivals ([Pulser device code](https://github.com/pasqal-io/Pulser/blob/develop/pulser-core/pulser/devices/_device_datacls.py); [Pulser crystals tutorial](https://github.com/pasqal-io/Pulser/blob/develop/tutorials/retired/Building%201D%20Rydberg%20Crystals.ipynb)). The physics ties to celebrated experiments ([Bernien et al.](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature24622); [Ebadi et al.](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-021-03582-4); [Turner et al.](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41567-018-0137-5)). *Inference:* the adiabatic sweep is the many-body form of the Landau–Zener problem that an *American Journal of Physics* project paper and a graduate course at Washington State use to show why numerics are needed ([Guttieres et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.11633); [WSU Physics 555](https://physics-555-quantum-technologies.readthedocs.io/en/fall2024/Notes/LandauZener.html)). The incumbents are strong and maintained at scale: Pulser shipped 1.9.0 and 1.9.1 in August and September 2026 ([Pulser releases](https://github.com/pasqal-io/Pulser/releases)), and its emulators cover about 25–27 atoms exactly, with an MPS emulator for larger arrays, while Bloqade.jl restricts to the blockade subspace and runs QMC to hundreds of atoms ([emulators](https://pasqal-io.github.io/emulators/latest/); [arXiv:2510.09813](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.09813); [subspace docs](https://queracomputing.github.io/Bloqade.jl/dev/subspace/)). The weakness is pedagogical, as the status table showed. Bloqade.jl's examples specify everything: C₆ = 2π×862,690 MHz µm⁶, giving 2π×7.33 MHz at 7 µm; a 9-atom chain at 5.72 µm with Ω = 2π×4 MHz and Δ swept from −2π×10 to +2π×10 MHz; a 21-atom quantum-link chain; and QMC benchmarked against exact diagonalisation on the 9-atom Z2 chain ([1.blockade](https://github.com/QuEraComputing/Bloqade.jl/blob/master/examples/1.blockade/main.jl); [2.adiabatic](https://github.com/QuEraComputing/Bloqade.jl/blob/master/examples/2.adiabatic/main.jl); [4.LGT](https://github.com/QuEraComputing/Bloqade.jl/blob/master/examples/4.LGT/main.jl); [7.QMC](https://github.com/QuEraComputing/Bloqade.jl/blob/master/examples/7.QMC/main.jl)). A persuasive study matches these numbers to the digits printed, shows full-space and blockade-subspace results agreeing (Bloqade advises a subspace radius below R_b), and wins on workflow with adiabaticity-versus-sweep-time and scar-versus-thermalisation studies that the retired tutorials no longer carry. It should not compete on atom count.

**7. Quench dynamics, light cones and Trotter error.** Light-cone spreading, linear entanglement growth and the cost of approximating them form the master's-level entry to many-body dynamics, and exact diagonalisation of spin chains is a standard graduate exercise ([ETH Computational Quantum Physics](https://edu.itp.phys.ethz.ch/fs11/cqp/cqp.pdf); [Quantumandu notes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.03564)). Incumbents serve it from three directions. quimb's TEBD example evolves a Heisenberg chain of L = 44 with two flipped spins to t = 80 and shows the ballistic light cone, entropy growth and a many-body-localised contrast at maximum bond dimensions of 15 and 19 (read through a summarising fetch) ([quimb TEBD example](https://quimb.readthedocs.io/en/latest/examples/ex_TEBD_evo.html)). PennyLane published *Exploring Trotterization* in July 2026, comparing first-order, Strang and Suzuki formulas on the single-qubit H = 0.2X + 1.3Z and citing commutator-scaling bounds ([PennyLane demo](https://pennylane.ai/qml/demos/exploring_trotterization); [Childs et al.](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevX.11.011020)). OpenFermion exposes `error_operator`, `error_bound` and `trotter_steps_required` ([OpenFermion Trotter module](https://github.com/quantumlib/OpenFermion/tree/master/src/openfermion/circuits/trotter)), IBM ships multi-product formulas specifically to reduce Trotter error ([qiskit-addon-mpf](https://github.com/Qiskit/qiskit-addon-mpf)), and the independent simulator benchmark uses a Trotterised XYZ Heisenberg chain as one of its three tasks ([Jamadagni et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.09076)). PennyLane's own Trotter-error estimator carries an open bug: it "should add up expectation values of nested commutators, not states" ([PennyLane #8152](https://github.com/PennyLaneAI/pennylane/issues/8152)). A persuasive study matches quimb's light cone and entropy curves, then supplies what the examples omit: an exact state-vector reference against which TEBD truncation error and Trotter error are measured and compared with commutator bounds, in one pipeline. *Inference:* chains of L ≈ 20–26 sit comfortably inside the roughly 30-qubit state-vector range every incumbent reaches, and the win here is workflow, not capability, because every ingredient exists among the incumbents, spread across three packages.

**8. Photonic Fock-space problems: lossy phase estimation and non-Gaussian gates.** Lossy interferometry asks which input state (coherent, squeezed plus coherent, NOON or optimised) measures a phase best at a given photon number N and transmission η, using exactly the master's toolkit of beam splitters, Fock and squeezed states, loss channels and Fisher information. The literature supplies both anchors and a twist: the lossy bound Δφ ≥ √((1−η)/(ηN)), and a canonical figure at η = 0.9 in which "the NOON states … achieve nearly optimal precision only for low N (≤10) and rapidly diverge becoming out-performed by classical strategies", while coherent plus squeezed light saturates the bound ([Demkowicz-Dobrzański et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1405.7703); [Caves](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.23.1693); [Dorner et al.](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.102.040403)), with squeezed light in LIGO as the real-world stake ([LIGO collaboration](https://doi.org/10.1038/nphoton.2013.177)). *Inference from absence:* no incumbent photonic tool showcases a worked lossy-interferometry or Fisher-information study; MrMustard's papers name metrology as an application without one. The general Fock tools truncate in incompatible ways (section one), and the cubic-phase gate exposes the cost sharply. A user reported Strawberry Fields errors that "prevent accurate simulation of even a single quartic phase gate" and overflow past cutoff 70, and staff replied they "won't be able to make improvements to the cubic phase gate" ([SF #753](https://github.com/XanaduAI/strawberryfields/issues/753)); a September 2026 preprint derived exact Fock amplitudes for any pure Gaussian input and found the truncated construction's error "at cutoffs in common use is found to be of the same size as the quantity being computed" ([arXiv:2609.13077](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13077)). A persuasive study reproduces the η = 0.9 curves with cutoff convergence certified, extends them to finite N and realistic η, and quantifies truncate-then-exponentiate error against the exact cubic-phase amplitudes. Two cautions bound it. The research did not establish which finite-N quantities already have closed forms, so the study must first show where numerics are indispensable. And the only library head-to-head available is Piquasso's CV neural-network protocol against Strawberry Fields (10 modes, 4 layers, probability loss near 10⁻²), which supports a match rather than a win ([Piquasso](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.04006v3)).

### Supporting studies establish parity on crowded ground

**9. Quantum trajectories and continuous measurement.** Monte Carlo unravelling "sheds light on the physical interpretation of the Lindblad master equation" and saves memory for large systems ([QuTiP 5 paper §3.2.7](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.04705)); Trinity's MSc assigns quantum-jump trajectories, and QuTiP teaches photon birth and death in a cavity and homodyne detection ([TCD worksheets](https://github.com/Ilumirnau/Open-Quantum-Systems); [notebook 006](https://nbviewer.org/urls/qutip.org/qutip-tutorials/tutorials-v5/time-evolution/006_photon_birth_death.ipynb); [notebook 022](https://nbviewer.org/urls/qutip.org/qutip-tutorials/tutorials-v5/time-evolution/022_v5_paper-smesolve.ipynb)). The incumbents are fast and well featured: QuantumToolbox.jl runs 100 Kerr trajectories in 0.018 s, QuTiP stops on a target statistical tolerance and samples the no-jump trajectory once, Dynamiqs batches jump and diffusive unravellings on GPUs, and qsim's delayed-inner-product algorithm gives "an order of magnitude speedup for low noise" ([QuTiP 5 paper §3.2.7](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.04705); [Dynamiqs jump measurement](https://www.dynamiqs.org/stable/documentation/advanced_examples/continuous-jump-measurement.html); [Isakov et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.02396)). The documented weak points are fine-grained: Dynamiqs warns that its event-based jump method produces "imprecise click times" unless the user bounds the step or supplies a root finder, and QuTiP's `smesolve` slowed more than tenfold in 5.2 ([Dynamiqs method.py](https://github.com/dynamiqs/dynamiqs/blob/main/dynamiqs/method.py); [QuTiP #2733](https://github.com/qutip/qutip/issues/2733)). A persuasive study matches trajectory averages to the master equation with error bars falling as one over the square root of the trajectory count, at timings near the published medians, and makes click-time precision and stochastic-integrator order visible. *Inference:* this is a supporting study because the Kerr benchmark already contains its Monte Carlo and stochastic legs.

**10. Which master equation: Lindblad, Bloch–Redfield or HEOM.** The QuTiP 5 paper shows why the question matters to a student: for a qubit whose energies are switched adiabatically, H = (Δ/2) sin(ω_d t) σ_z, Bloch–Redfield and HEOM "capture this switching effect automatically", while "the naive approach, however, using a single constant collapse operator σ− fails and is insensitive to the drive" ([QuTiP 5 paper §3.2.4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.04705)). HEOM also shows the zero-temperature localisation transition of the spin-boson model, and a *PRX Quantum* tutorial teaches Lindblad, Redfield and Floquet forms to graduate students ([QuTiP 5 paper §3.2.12](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.04705); [Campaioli et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.16449)). Only QuTiP, through BoFiN, and HierarchicalEOM.jl offer numerically exact HEOM, and HierarchicalEOM.jl reports "a significant speedup" over QuTiP's implementation ([BoFiN](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.10806); [HierarchicalEOM.jl](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.07522)); QuantumOptics.jl stops at Bloch–Redfield and Dynamiqs offers neither. The weaknesses are at research level: QuTiP's ESPRIT fit fails for long-memory baths such as a giant atom, and Bloch–Redfield does not guarantee positivity ([QuTiP #2950](https://github.com/qutip/qutip/issues/2950); [arXiv:2402.06354](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.06354)). A persuasive study matches the paper's Fig. 3 failure and the spin-boson tutorial curves, with convergence in hierarchy depth and number of bath exponents shown ([HEOM tutorial 1a](https://nbviewer.org/urls/qutip.org/qutip-tutorials/tutorials-v5/heom/heom-1a-spin-bath-model-basic.ipynb)). It should not contest HEOM throughput, and the BoFiN finding that "the Uhrig pulse-spacing scheme is less optimal than equally spaced pulses when the environment's spectral density is very broad" is a stretch target for a later study ([BoFiN](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.10806)).

**11. Characterization as inference: T1, T2, RB and tomography.** Characterization turns open-system physics into statistics (T1, Ramsey and echo fits, randomized-benchmarking decays, tomography under positivity constraints), and UMass teaches it in Qiskit labs ([UMass ECE 550/650](https://github.com/UMassIonTrappers/Introduction-to-Quantum-Computing)). qiskit-experiments is the active bar, with fit models and noise parameters in its manuals: RB under depolarizing errors of 5×10⁻³ on single-qubit gates and 5×10⁻² on CX, reporting EPC, the decay parameter α and EPG, and T1 and Tphi under FakePerth thermal relaxation ([RB manual](https://qiskit-community.github.io/qiskit-experiments/manuals/verification/randomized_benchmarking.html); [T1 manual](https://qiskit-community.github.io/qiskit-experiments/manuals/characterization/t1.html)). Cirq supplies parallel helpers and XEB ([Cirq experiments module](https://github.com/quantumlib/Cirq/tree/main/cirq-core/cirq/experiments)). The noise models behind these manuals are "only an *approximation* of the real errors", built from average error rates ([Aer device noise](https://qiskit.github.io/qiskit-aer/tutorials/2_device_noise_simulation.html)). A persuasive study matches EPC, EPG, T1 and T2 from the same noise models within fit uncertainty, then wins on workflow by generating the data from the pulse-level Lindblad model of study 2 rather than from per-gate average channels.

**12. Molecular dissociation of H2 and LiH: validation only.** These are the most widely shared gate-model benchmarks and they come with exact references: −1.1373060483 Ha for H2 in PennyLane's differentiable Hartree–Fock demo, −7.8825378193 Ha for LiH, and a chemical-accuracy target of 0.0016 Ha ([HF demo](https://pennylane.ai/qml/demos/tutorial_differentiable_HF); [adaptive circuits](https://pennylane.ai/qml/demos/tutorial_adaptive_circuits); [geometry optimisation](https://pennylane.ai/qml/demos/tutorial_mol_geo_opt)). OpenFermion prints HF, MP2 and FCI energies along an H2 STO-3G curve from 0.3 to 2.5 Å but loads them from files shipped with the package, and its PySCF and Psi4 plugins have not been released since December 2020 ([OpenFermion intro](https://github.com/quantumlib/OpenFermion/blob/master/docs/tutorials/intro_to_openfermion.ipynb); [OpenFermion-PySCF](https://github.com/quantumlib/OpenFermion-PySCF/releases)). *Inference:* the H2 STO-3G qubit Hamiltonian under Jordan–Wigner is a 16×16 matrix, so numerics barely decide the answer; a QF study should match these values to micro-hartree precision as a correctness check and use the fermion-to-qubit mapping as an on-ramp to study 7, not present it as a numerical showcase.

### Arenas to concede or defer

Five arenas reward hardware and specialist kernels rather than numerical judgement. Exact Gaussian boson sampling runs on supercomputers: a 100×100 hafnian would need "the 288000 CPUs of this machine for about a month and a half" ([Björklund et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.12498)). Large-distance threshold sweeps under Pauli noise are Stim's home ground, with kilohertz sampling and sub-microsecond decoding per round (table above). Random-circuit and 127-qubit utility contractions sit at 10¹⁴–10²² FLOPs or effective bond dimensions above 1.6×10⁷ ([Gray & Kourtis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.01935); [Begušić et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.05077)). Full Lindblad simulation of dissipative Ising chains tops out at 10–12 spins and about 10³ s in every library, which makes it a GPU-memory contest ([QuantumToolbox.jl §6](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.21440); [QuTiP 5 paper §3.2.5](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.04705)). Variational-algorithm studies (QAOA MaxCut, barren plateaus, natural gradients) are PennyLane's deepest pedagogy and teach optimisation more than quantum mechanics, and PennyLane's own development branch is deleting its `qaoa` module ([changelog-dev](https://github.com/PennyLaneAI/pennylane/blob/main/doc/releases/changelog-dev.md)). Collective-spin problems such as superradiance and the open Dicke model deserve deferral rather than concession: they need permutation symmetry or cumulant expansions to scale, and a QuTiP bug giving a wrong PIQS Liouvillian spectrum has been open since 2019 ([PIQS paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.05129); [QuTiP #993](https://github.com/qutip/qutip/issues/993)). *Inference:* small-instance correctness checks in any of these arenas make useful appendices, but none should carry a study's argument.

## A persuasive study reports error, versions and cost side by side

The published comparisons show how easily a head-to-head misleads. In the QuantumToolbox.jl benchmark, Dynamiqs' "mcsolve" is a fixed-step jump unravelling rather than adaptive Monte Carlo, QuTiP's `smesolve` time may include a regression that was being fixed when the benchmark ran, and Dynamiqs computes in single precision unless told otherwise ([Dynamiqs benchmark script](https://github.com/albertomercurio/QuantumToolbox.jl-Paper-Figures/blob/main/src/benchmarks/python/dynamiqs_benchmarks.py); [QuTiP #2733](https://github.com/qutip/qutip/issues/2733); [Dynamiqs sharp bits](https://www.dynamiqs.org/stable/documentation/getting_started/sharp-bits.html)). Jamadagni et al. found two packages running double precision despite documented single-precision support ([Jamadagni et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.09076)). Threshold numbers differ by normalisation as well as by noise model: Stim's "roughly 1%" is a per-round figure under uniform noise, while the SD6 range is per block of d rounds ([Stim notebook](https://github.com/quantumlib/Stim/blob/main/doc/getting_started.ipynb); [Gidney et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.10457)). Every match leg should therefore pin package versions, arithmetic precision, tolerances, integration method and normalisation, and print them beside each number.

The stronger differentiator is reporting accuracy together with cost. No incumbent open-system benchmark scores error against a converged reference; the closest precedents are Piquasso's probability loss versus cutoff, Dynamiqs' rejected-step counts at discontinuities, and the truncation thresholds that approximate Pauli-path simulators report ([Piquasso](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.04006v3); [Dynamiqs benchmarks](https://github.com/dynamiqs/dynamiqs/blob/main/benchmarks/README.md); [Mazumder et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09882)). Rigorous, computable a-posteriori bounds on both Hilbert-space truncation and time discretisation of Lindblad simulations were published in 2026 and enable "fully adaptive simulations", yet the research found no sign that QuTiP, QuantumToolbox.jl, QuantumOptics.jl or Dynamiqs implements them ([Etienney, Robin & Rouchon](https://quantum-journal.org/papers/q-2026-03-16-2031/)). *Inference:* a study that plots error against wall time, with convergence demonstrated in cutoff, tolerance and precision, would set a standard no incumbent has published.

Timing still matters, but it should not carry the argument. At teaching sizes every incumbent finishes in seconds, and the large spreads come from per-call overhead: a single-author study found hand-written C beating QuTiP 5.2.3 by up to two orders of magnitude on three-level Lindblad propagation, an advantage that had inverted by dimension 27 ([Malarchick](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.18052)). Reproducibility over time is a separate and documented frustration: scqubits' composite-system workflows broke under QuTiP 5.3.1, a second derivative in the QuTiP 5 paper's own JAX tutorial has failed since JAX 0.7.0, and Strawberry Fields' last release supports only Python 3.8 to 3.10 ([scqubits #320](https://github.com/scqubits/scqubits/issues/320); [qutip-jax #95](https://github.com/qutip/qutip-jax/issues/95); [Strawberry Fields README](https://github.com/XanaduAI/strawberryfields)). *Inference:* studies that re-execute against pinned versions, and that treat convergence as visible content rather than a hidden step, answer two documented frustrations at once; the syllabi examined never list truncation or tolerance convergence as a topic, so the convergence plots double as teaching material.

## Conclusion

The competitive opening is not numerical versus symbolic computation but verified numerics versus fast numerics. The incumbents compete on GPU throughput, batching and gradients, measured at fixed settings by the packages that win, while their users file the same accuracy failures year after year; pulse skipping alone has drawn reports since 2020. A study series that reproduces each incumbent's own published number and then certifies convergence where that incumbent's tracker records failure would occupy ground nobody claims, and because every claim in it is numerical, it answers the "symbolic only" verdict directly rather than by assertion.

The window is narrowing. QuantumToolbox.jl added adaptive Fock truncation in 2025 and an arbitrary-precision guide in July 2026, Dynamiqs now counts rejected steps in its benchmark suite, and scqubits has a convergence-diagnostics module under review ([QuantumToolbox.jl paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.21440); [precision guide](https://qutip.org/QuantumToolbox.jl/stable/users_guide/arbitrary_precision); [Dynamiqs benchmarks](https://github.com/dynamiqs/dynamiqs/blob/main/benchmarks/README.md); [scqubits #306](https://github.com/scqubits/scqubits/issues/306)). *Inference:* the incumbents are beginning to compete on accuracy, and the orphaned niches of pulse-level transmon dynamics and Fock-space photonics will not stay empty, so the first convergence-checked worked studies on the Kerr oscillator and the multilevel transmon are likely to become the reference that later tools are measured against. What remains unknown is how a master's audience weighs these topics against one another; no source in the research measured it, and a short survey of the audience that judged the earlier showcase would test this ranking cheaply.
